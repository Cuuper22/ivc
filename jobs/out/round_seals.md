## Terms in plain words

- **Round seal**: Lipi catalogue type SEAL:C (a round stamp, 32 faces) or SEAL:CY (a cylinder, 9 faces). 41 faces on **39 objects** (objects 1388 and 2419 have two faces each). **Square seal**: any other seal.
- **Where**: *home* = Indus region (15 faces, 13 objects); *abroad* = Gulf, Mesopotamia, Iran (18); *unknown site* = round seals with no findspot (7); *S India* = Daimabad (1, one sign).
- **Sign**: one drawn character, by Wells/Lipi number. **Person signs**: 090 (one man), 091 (two men side by side), 093 (man with a small stroke between the legs), 095 (man with a short stick). **Stroke signs**: the counting strokes (001-005, 031-036, 055 four stacked short strokes, 013 and the other stroke classes of frame v2). **Short stroke**: 001-005 and 055. **Tall stroke**: 031-036.
- **Reading order**: the order the signs are read; every text here is written in that order. A **gap** is a lost (000), unclear (ALT) or never-seen sign; it breaks "next to".
- **Bits per step**: how surprising the next sign is to a model trained on home texts; lower is more predictable. **Shuffle**: the same signs of each text rearranged at random inside that text. **Matched square seals**: for each round text, a random home square-seal text with the same number of signs. **P**: the share of shuffles or draws at least as extreme as the real count; small P means chance does not give it.
- **Closer**: a sign that ends a home name text (jar 740, spear 520, the carrier signs). **Impression**: clay pressed with a seal and left on goods (`work_data/stamps/impressions.csv`, 336 faces, all at home sites).

## What was found

1. **Round seals hold one unit that the rest of the script almost never has: a short stroke right before a person sign.** `[001][090]`, `[055][090]`, `[001][091]`, `[001][093]` stand in 6 of 37 round texts with two or more signs (Failaka 148.1, Bahrain 3863.1, unknown-site 3888.1 and 5223.1, Saar 5229.1, and the home round seal of Dholavira 4247.1). In the 3,426 distinct non-round home texts (seals, tablets, tags, pots) the unit occurs once (Kalibangan square seal 1898.1, `002-093`); that one is among 2,002 distinct home seal texts, so 1 of 2,002 against 6 of 37 (Fisher P 1.6e-10). The reverse order, person then short stroke, is on 3 of 3,426 at home (`work_data/round/log_9.txt`). Five of the six are abroad or unknown-site seals and one was found at Dholavira; five are stamps and one (3888.1) is a cylinder. What it means: round seals have their own phrase, and it is a number-then-person phrase, one man or a small count of men. What it opens: the unit is a search key for round-seal texts in every catalogue not yet read (see Gulf section) and the first fixed phrase of the round class.
2. **A person sign and a stroke stand next to each other in 9 of the 10 round texts that hold both.** Shuffled inside each text, 5.5 of 10 would (P 0.011; `log_2b.txt`). Either order is found: stroke then person 6, person then stroke 5. The tall strokes come after twin men: `091-031` (Karzakan 5227.1) and `091-032` (Karzakan 5228.1), two of the three Karzakan seals. The short strokes come before: 001 four times, 055 twice, never 002-005. What it means: the round text is built from person-and-number units; the order of the units depends on the stroke height. What it opens: a test for a tally reading: the strokes should run 1, 2, 3 and so on; here they are single stroke or four stacked, which is what the next collection of round seals must settle.
3. **The man sign does not do its home job on round seals.** At home 090 is the last sign of the text in 61 of 81 uses (75%; it is the after-jar title). On round seals it is last in 2 of 10 (Fisher P 0.001); it is first 2, middle 6. Twin men 091 stay in their home job: first in 5 of 8 round uses (4 of 5 at home), last in 0 of 8 (home 0 of 5). Pattern `[ordinary sign][person]` with a person closing the text, the "X's man" order, is in 0 of 37 round texts (matched square seals 1.33). What it means: reading 090 as a title after a name does not carry over to round seals.
4. **Round seals share no first sign, no last sign and no fixed run; persons and strokes sit where a shuffle puts them.** First sign shared by pairs of round texts: 6 pairs against 13.65 in matched square seals (P of fewer 0.11); last sign: 4 pairs against 81.2 (P of fewer 0.0005, since square seals end in closers and round seals do not). Middle sign shared by a pair: 106 against 111.4 in square seals (no excess; the apparent excess over a redeal of all signs, 66.5, is a false lead: lost signs sit at the edges of real texts and would fall in the middle of a redeal). Position of person signs and strokes inside the text against the shuffle: 090 first/middle/last 2/6/2 (shuffle 2.4/5.2/2.4); strokes 8/33/5 (shuffle 9.0/28.1/8.9); 091 3/3/0 over 6 uses (shuffle 1.3/3.3/1.3; `structure_positions.csv`). Nothing sits where it would not by shuffle except the 091 front and the absent closers.
5. **Home round seals and foreign round seals are different in people and animal, alike in strokes.** Person signs 2 of 56 signs at home against 18 of 97 abroad and unknown-site (3.6% against 18.6%, Fisher P 0.011); the man 090 alone 0 of 56 against 10 of 97 (P 0.014); texts holding a person sign 2 of 15 against 17 of 25 (P 0.001). Strokes 28.6% against 30.9% (P 0.86). The short-horned bull is on 3 of 15 home faces against 16 of 25 (P 0.0098); the home round seals carry the composite beast, no beast, a unicorn or a scene, the animals of the home glyptic. Home round seals are not clustered: 8 of 15 faces at Mohenjo-daro (home seals 60.6%), spread over DKE 3, DKG south 3, HRB 1, VSA 1; the other 7 at Harappa 3, Dholavira, Chanhu-daro, Kalibangan, Rakhigarhi; time middle 7 and late 4 (4 not binned), early none (home seals: middle 47.7%, late 40.3%, early 11.9%). Of the 15 home faces, 6 are cylinders (scene seals and plain-field cylinders); of the 25 foreign, 3. The two groups share 12 signs but only one adjacent pair, `001-091`. What it means: the home round seals are two things: the Dholavira stamp 4247.1 is a Gulf-type seal at home (it carries the unit of finding 1 with a bull), the rest are home glyptic (cylinders, composite beasts) whose texts do not hold the person units. The same round shape does not mean the same text.
6. **No home object kind resembles the round seals; the all-kinds model is as good as any.** Sign-pair models per kind, all trained without any round seal, scored on 33 round texts (107 steps): all-kinds 8.99 bits per step; tags and sealings 8.78 (a size-matched all-kinds model gives 8.94 [8.67, 9.21], P 0.12 that the tags are lower by chance), all seals 9.11 (8.94), pots 9.11 (8.88), name seals 9.39 (8.90), tablets 9.42 (8.89), count-line seals 9.66 (9.26), copper tablets 9.63 (9.07). The prediction (tablets or count-line seals closest) is wrong: they are among the worst. Class-level redesign (frame v2 rules learned per kind): the gap between round seals and matched square seals is 2.21 bits under pooled rules, 2.31 under seal rules, 2.27 tags, 2.36 tablets, 2.53 copper tablets, 1.94 pots; every P is 0.002. What it means: pots come nearest, by 0.27 bits, which is a thin edge on a gap of two bits: round seals are not a sub-kind of a home object.
7. **Round seals touch clay impressions less than square seals do, with one exact meeting.** Whole text equal to an impression: 1 of 37 (Kalibangan cylinder 1924.1, `003-390`, equal to Mohenjo-daro impression 2408.1; matched square seals 0.23). An adjacent pair in the same order found in an impression: 8 of 37 against 21.0 [16, 27] for matched square seals (P of fewer 0.0005): 148.1 `350-001` (Kalibangan 1944.3), 1388.1 `705-033` (4 impressions: Kalibangan, Lothal, Harappa) and `741-031`, 1388.2 `033-700`, 1924.1 `003-390`, 3668.1 `820-060` (Lothal), 5224.1 `033-220` (two Harappa impressions) and `001-032`, 5229.1 `055-220` (11 impressions, the Lothal `705-500-741-001-055-220-740-090` among them), 5231.1 `090-405` (Harappa 940.1, 942.1). The unit pairs of finding 1 meet no impression, and of the 6 unit seals only 148.1 and 5229.1 share any other pair with one. What it means: the home round seals (1388, 1924, 3668) share home pairs and meet impressions; the person-and-number seals mostly do not.
8. **The Gulf published view agrees on shape and place and disagrees on text.** Gulf round stamp seals with short Indus inscriptions appear at Bahrain around 2050 BC; round stamps with the short-horned bull at Failaka and Bahrain; a late 3rd-millennium cylinder (Jalalabad seal) joins them. Dating: our catalogue puts the foreign square seals earlier (Kish 1970.1, 1971.1: Akkadian; Tello 3885.1: 3rd millennium) and the dated round ones later or at the end of the 3rd millennium (Ur 3898.1 Dynasty III; Susa 3881.1, 3882.1 late 3rd millennium; Failaka 147.1, 148.1 and unknown-site 3888.1 about 2000 BC; Bahrain 3863.1 period IIa). Round seals sit later than the square ones abroad. Details, links and the match to our ids below.

## The Gulf seals, as published

I read the free summaries; I could not read the full papers, so no count of Gulf-type seals with Indus signs is stated by any page I could open, and I give none.

- Laursen 2010 ([summary](https://www.harappa.com/blog/story-gulf-type-indus-seal), [article page](https://www.harappa.com/content/westward-transmission-indus-valley-sealing-technology-origin-and-development)): round Gulf-type seals grew out of Indus sealing as Harappans who had left the main cities worked in the west; the early Dilmun phase is put at about 2100-2000 BC and the type is tied to Dilmun merchants from about 2050 BC. The distribution list runs over Bahrain, Failaka, Ur, Girsu, Susa, Luristan, Kerman and also Mohenjo-daro and Chanhu-daro. Of the texts, the page says the Indus inscriptions carry unusual prefixed twin signs and a new seal with a pseudo-inscription, and that the signs probably write a language other than the Harappan one. The twin-sign remark may be our 091 (twin men) at the front; I have not checked that against the paper: 091 is first in 5 of 8 round uses and its home count is 11.
- A review of the Babylonia-Gulf-Indus evidence ([page](https://www.harappa.com/content/babylonia-gulf-region-and-indus-archaeological-and-textual-evidence-contact-third-and-early)): round Gulf-type stamps with short inscriptions appear at Qala'at al-Bahrain about 2050 BC; only some carry writing; spread from Babylonia to Sindh and Gujarat (Dholavira).
- Vidale, short-horned bull ([page](https://www.harappa.com/content/short-horned-bull-indus-seals-symbol-families-western-trade)): the bull is under 5% of standard Indus seals (the unicorn about 58%), rare on the few round seals of Mohenjo-daro and Chanhu-daro, and dominant on round seals in Mesopotamia; at least eight Gulf round seals at Failaka and Bahrain carry it; he proposes that early Mesopotamian-town seals copied square Indus seals with cuneiform added and that later, people of Indian origin in the west cut round steatite stamps, perhaps writing a local language with Indus signs. Bead trade families may have used the bull as a mark.
- Jalalabad seal ([abstract](https://jarcs.ut.ac.ir/article_81265.html)): a cylinder, late 3rd millennium, reportedly from Fars, scene plus a run of Indus signs, linked to short Indus-sign inscriptions on Persian Gulf round stamps from Bahrain and southern Mesopotamia; the second half of the 3rd millennium.
- Dilmun burials ([page](https://www.harappa.com/content/dilmun-meluhhan-relations-revisited-light-observations-early-dilmun-seal-production-during)): Bahrain seals of about 2050-1800 BC were worn at the neck, linked to children, passed down generations, and seem to have circulated across most of the population, unlike the elite Indus seals. Our Gulf round seals come from cemeteries: Karzakan (3), Saar, Janabiyah.
- A Failaka and Bahrain photo set ([page](https://www.harappa.com/node/4078)): one Bahrain seal (Kjaerum 1994, fig. 1725) and two Failaka (Kjaerum 1983, nos. 279 and 319), all bull and Indus signs.

Match to our ids (by site, nothing stronger): the Bahrain bull seal is Lipi 3863.1 (the only Bahrain round seal); the two Failaka seals are 147.1 and 148.1 (the only two at Failaka; which number is 279 and which 319 is not settled). Of the "at least eight" bull round seals of the Gulf, our catalogue has six with the bull symbol at Failaka, Bahrain, Karzakan (3), Janabiyah; the gap to eight is not reconciled. Dholavira 4247.1, the home round stamp with the bull, is the one the Gulf-type distribution list reaches. Dating against the square seals: the published view puts Gulf-type round seals from about 2050 BC, the foreign square seals in our catalogue at Kish are Akkadian, earlier; round seals sit alongside the end of the square run or after it. Published view of their texts: a language other than Harappan (Laursen), a local language written with Indus signs (Vidale); neither is tested by data in what I could read. Our data agrees on the three points that can be counted: round Gulf seals have a text of their own (finding 1, 4), twin men at the front (finding 3), and no home name-and-closer shape (0 of 9 intact end in a closer, expected 6.3).

## Which of (a), (b), (c), (d) the round seals are

Working position, committed: **the round stamps abroad (and the Dholavira stamp) are a local record made with Indus signs: lists of persons with a count, in person-and-number units (finding 1 to 3), made in the Gulf-type workshops (d), and not a name system of the home kind.** Building on that.

- (a) Personal seals of people outside the home naming system. For: no name-and-closer shape (0 of 9 intact end in a closer, 6.3 expected), no home phrase (1 of 18 against 8.4), the man sign not in its title job (2 of 10 last against 61 of 81). Against: the form "X's man" (person last after an ordinary sign) in 0 of 37, so an owner-name-plus-"man" reading is not there; and a personal seal would not give the number-then-person unit (6 of 37 against 1 of 3,426). Alive, as the reading of the texts that have no unit (most with 091 first, e.g. `091-840-413-831`, `091-031-455-220`).
- (b) Tally of persons. For: unit `[short stroke][person]` 6 of 37 against 1 of 3,426; person and stroke adjacent in 9 of the 10 texts that hold both (5.5 expected, P 0.011). Against: the plain patterns `[person][stroke]`, `[stroke][person]`, `[other][person]` at the end counted together reach 9 texts against 7.7 when each text is shuffled (P 0.31), so ordered adjacency beyond the stock is only what finding 2 says; the strokes before the person are 001 (4) or 055 (2), no 2, 3, 4. What would settle it: seals where the stroke before the person varies 1, 2, 3 in a fixed place; the Kjaerum Failaka and Bahrain catalogues and the Susa pieces are the place to read.
- (c) The signs used for another language. For: the published view (Laursen), the twin men in front. Against: no run or pair is shared in fixed order across round seals (a pair model trained on the other round seals does worse than sign frequencies: -0.35 bits per step against +1.06 for home sets; 0 shared runs in 41 core texts), and the sound-spelling check went against (less flat). What would settle it: three or more seals sharing the same ordered run of three signs. None so far.
- (d) A local Gulf product imitating Indus signs. For: the text type is the same at a Dholavira house and at Gulf cemeteries (the unit), the shape is a known local class, the dates are later than the square seals, no home object kind explains the texts. Against: the two round seals with scenes at Kalibangan and Harappa are not Gulf-type; the signs on the photographed home pieces are drawn the home way; nothing shows the carving of a single foreign piece (no photograph reached me). What would settle it: the carving quality of the foreign pieces (photographs of the 26 without one) and whether a sign on them differs in drawing from home signs.

## What went against

- The tally patterns A, B and C counted together equal their shuffles (9 against 7.7, P 0.31); "X's man" is in 0 of 37. Redesign (condition on texts holding both a person and a stroke) turned it around: 9 of 10 adjacent against 5.5 (P 0.011), and the unit search found 6 of 37 against 1 of 3,426. The tally reading stays alive, not proved: strokes never count above one or four.
- Raw sign-pair models per object kind: tags 8.78 against a size-matched 8.94 (P 0.12), nothing significant, tablets and count-line seals among the worst; redesigned with job classes: every gap 1.9 to 2.5 bits, P 0.002. No kind is a relative. The sample is 33 texts and 107 steps; the pair-model numbers are noisy because the kinds are small.
- The middle-sign excess over a redeal (106 against 66.5, P 0.002) is not a finding: lost signs sit at the ends of real texts. Against matched square seals it is 106 against 111.4.
- Pierced, boss, steatite and size could not be compared home against abroad: the catalogue leaves them blank for most foreign records, so the apparent differences (steatite 13 of 15 home against 5 of 25) are record-keeping, not the stones.
- No foreign photograph was obtainable, so the questions "one line or round the animal" and "drawn the home way" are answered for 11 home pieces only.
- The round class mixes 9 cylinder faces (6 at home) with 32 round stamps. Five of the six unit seals are stamps; 3888.1 is a cylinder.
