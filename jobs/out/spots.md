# Spots: the house manager, the quarter-office, the tablets as records

Word-free job. Signs are named by number and picture only. Pictures used (from `work_data/sign_shapes.csv`): 740 jar; 090 two-armed person ("man"); 142 person with staff; 061 reversed lambda; 820 six-spoke wheel (061 and 820 are the two "quarter" signs); 400 comb (vertical line with side ticks); 176 seated person with four ticks on the back; 700 plain U ("cup"); 705/706 U with a long stroke through the gap ("big measure"); 741/742/745 jar with 1/2/3 strokes in the mouth ("ward signs"); 617 and 615 are other name signs.

Scripts (in `work_scripts/`, predictions written in each top docstring before any number): `spots_1_manager.py`, `spots_1b_rescue.py`, `spots_2_office.py`, `spots_2b_spread.py`, `spots_3_records.py`, `spots_3b_rescue.py`. Tables and printouts in `work_data/spots/` (`s1_*`, `s2_*`, `s2b_*`, `s3_*`; each script's log is `sN_log.txt`).

Plain words used below:
- **Crest** = first name sign (family emblem). **Master** = the person named before the jar in a jar+man seal. **Manager** = a seal whose text ends jar 740 + man 090; the master's crest is the name before the jar. **Owner object** = any undamaged object with a name closed by an ending other than jar+man, carrying the master's crest.
- **Building / block / area (trench) / mound**: findspot levels. Building = excavation area + house (Mohenjo-daro) or + grid square (elsewhere); block = Mohenjo-daro block number or Harappa grid row; area = the catalogue's excavation area (a trench at Harappa); mound = Harappa letter group (F, E, ET, AB, D).
- **Shuffle**: the thing in question (a findspot, a crest label, an amount) is swapped at random inside the same city many thousand times; **P** = share of shuffles at least as strong as the real count. Small P = not chance, pointing the stated way.
- **Stand letter** = W, J, S, I, L after the colon on unicorn seals, the household mark (room-mates share it 38% against 17%).
- **Face / record**: one inscribed side; a multi-face tablet is one record. **Face type** (job only): *name* face (has a name job: crest, owner ending, opener or tag), *amount* face (only count signs, cup 700 and other measures), *thing* face (word-signs that are neither), *lost* (all signs broken).

## 1. What was found

### Lead 1. The manager lives in the master's building, and is a bigger seal, at Mohenjo-daro

1. **Mohenjo-daro result reruns exactly and strengthens one level up.** Managers found in a building that also holds an owner object of the master's crest: 5 of 24 against 2.09 shuffled (ratio 2.39, P 0.044). At block level 9 of 24 against 5.28 (ratio 1.71, P 0.044). At whole-area level 17 of 26 against 15.5 (chance). Family members (non-manager owner seals) at chance at every level (building 44 of 588). Pooled over all cities: building 5 of 30 against 2.33 (ratio 2.14, P 0.072); block 11 of 30 against 6.35 (ratio 1.73, P 0.024). `s1_a_sites.csv`. Means: the manager is tied to the master's own building and its immediate block, not to the master's wider quarter. Opens: a manager seal marks where the master's family lived, so a manager seal can date and place a house.

2. **The manager's building is a main house.** Over the 27 managers whose master crest has owner objects in the same city, the master-crest objects found in the manager's own building total 9, against 2.94 shuffled (ratio 3.06, P 0.0104). The 5 hits and where the building ranks among all places holding that crest in the city: M-100 (crest 705) rank 1 of 22, M-265 (crest 061) rank 1 of 12, M-865 (crest 235) rank 1 of 33, M-199 (crest 240) rank 2 of 23, M-1746 (crest 240) rank 4 of 23. Three of 27 managers sit in the single biggest holder of their crest, against 25 of 588 ordinary owner seals (11% against 4.3%; Fisher P 0.12). Means: when a manager sits with the master, it is where the crest's objects pile up, but the piles are small (1 to 3 objects). At area level no pull (76 against 70.9). `s1_b_mainhouse.csv`.

3. **Managers' seals are bigger inside Mohenjo-daro and pooled across sites.** Mohenjo-daro: 33 managers, median face 778 mm2, against 629 for 833 other seals (P 0.027) and 639 for 208 owner seals of the same crests (P 0.047). Pooled over 5 sites with 45 managers, a manager's seal sits at the 60.2th percentile of its own site's sizes against 50.3 by label shuffle inside the site (P 0.013). Managers 758 mm2 against 645 for plain "house of" owner seals (P 0.034). Lothal: 2 managers, median 881 against 412 (P 0.09). `s1_c_sizes.csv`.

4. **Managers use a different stand mix: mid stands S and J, not the big stand W.** Mohenjo-daro: 21 managers carry a stand letter: S 8, J 7, W 5, I 1, L 0. Among all 501 unicorn seals with a stand letter at Mohenjo-daro W is 39%, J 25%, S 22%. S plus J: 15 of 21 (71%) against 47% (binomial P 0.021). W is only 5 of 21 even though W is the biggest-format stand (median 930 mm2 against 587 for S) and managers' seals are bigger than average. Means: the manager is marked out in the household-mark system as its own class, not merged into W, the big stand carried by the great houses (W leads in High-star, Bamboo-star, Home, Descendant per `registry.md`). Opens: the stand letter may encode rank within the household; test it against the other endings (spear 520, comb 400) next. `s1b_log.txt`.

### Lead 2. Sign 061 before 142

5. **A count stands directly in front of the quarter sign in the pair's texts.** Pair texts (061 or 820, then 142): 11 in Lipi, 0 more in the 1,183 new second-catalogue texts. A count run sits immediately before the quarter sign in 5 of 11 pair texts (45%) against 36 of 379 (9%) for other texts with a quarter sign (one-sided P 0.0029). The counts: 033 (three tall strokes) at H-1672, 002 (two short strokes) at M-1772, M-164 and M-717, 031 (one tall stroke) at M-837. The text ends in the jar in 10 of 11 pair texts against 126 of 379 (33%; P 0.0002). Means: the pair is a fixed phrase `[count] [061] [142] ... [jar]` with a count slot: "the first / second / third" something, which is the form a numbered office takes (numbered quarters). Opens: numbered offices; the count values 1, 2, 3 are the first values for a numbered-quarter list, and any text with `[count] 061 142` can be asked which quarter number sits where it was found.

6. **The lineage text is one formula in two cities.** `061-142-400-740` (quarter, staff-person, comb, jar) is on H-1679, H-451, H-780 and M-879, identical sign for sign; counted or prefixed variants are H-1672 (`033-061-142-400-740`) and H-471 (`368-061-142-400-740`). All Harappa copies are dated middle bin; the holder sign after 142 is the comb 400 in all 5 dated Harappa objects. `s2_office_objects.csv`.

### Lead 3. Tablets as records

7. **Record kinds (902 objects).** amount+name 356 (40%; 43% of the 832 two-face objects), name+thing 176, amount+thing 94, name+name 88, thing+thing 41, amount+amount+thing 24, amount+name+thing 9, name+name+thing 6, lost+name 29, amount+lost 20, lost+thing 20. Harappa 750 objects: amount+name 343, name+thing 120, amount+thing 92, name+name 56. Mohenjo-daro 107: name+thing 51, name+name 20, thing+thing 13, amount+name only 5. So the name-plus-amount tablet is a Harappa kind; Mohenjo-daro tablets are name plus a thing-face or two names. By trench: Harappa FTrench I 57 of 90 are amount+name, ETrench 54 27 of 46, ETrench 10W/11 22 of 48, D 20 of 35; ETrench 11 is different (amount+name 33 of 130 but amount+amount+thing 21 and amount+thing 16): the E11 trench holds the three-part "hands / loads / cups" tablet. Copper tablets (40) are name+thing 31. `s3_a_kind_by_site.csv`, `s3_a_kind_by_trench.csv`.

8. **A name keeps its own amount; batches share the name while the amount steps.** Three batches of 5 or more identical tablets (no lost signs, one site and trench): Harappa ETrench 11, 20 copies of `003-156 | 003-861 | 034-700` (all incised); Harappa FTrench I, 15 copies of `033-700 | 176-740-400` (moulded, incised and a round seal) and 7 copies of `034-700 | 176-740-400` (incised). The two FTrench I batches share the name face (176 seated person, jar, comb) and change the amount from 3 cups to 4 cups, which is the "same name, amount changes" pattern. Across every signature (1+ copies) in the same trench, pairs that share the name and differ in amount are 9 against 3.5 shuffled (P 0.011); pairs that share the amount and differ in name are at chance (282 against 275.6). Counting each name+amount+trench once (256 triples from 290 records) pairs of triples with one name carry the same amount 38 times against 21.6 shuffled (P 0.003): a name keeps a typical amount. 25 of 46 names on two or more records carry two or more amounts; 176-740-400 alone has 31 records with amounts of 2, 3 and 4 cups (3 cups on 20, 4 on 8, 2 on 2) in 5 trenches. Means: tablets are entries of standing accounts, one holder with a usual ration and sometimes a step above or below. Opens: the 3- versus 4-cup step on one holder's tablets is the first place to look for a rule (rank, month, season).

9. **The cup ceiling holds on every record.** Cup counts on the 902 records: 1 on 3, 2 on 97, 3 on 146, 4 on 121, 6 on 2; none is 5 and none above 6. Records with a cup count and the big measure (705/706): 2 in the whole catalogue (object 1211 Harappa: 3 cups and 3 big measures on one face; object 1388: 3 cups and 1 big measure, on two faces), both far below the 12-cups-to-one ceiling. Means: 12 cups = 1 big measure is not contradicted by any record in the multi-face set; the ladder stays intact in the one place cups and the big measure meet. `s3_d_cups.csv`.

## 2. What went against, and the rescue tried

**Lead 1 (a), Harappa and the small sites.** Harappa: 0 of 6 managers in a building with a master-crest object (shuffle 0.20), block 2 of 6 against 1.06 (P 0.28), trench 3 of 7 against 1.97 (P 0.29), mound 5 of 7 against 3.89 (P 0.29). Kalibangan and Dholavira have no manager read whole (0); Lothal 2, with an area but no building (0 of 2). Rescue 1 (redesign): count managers of every object kind (tags, tablets, not only seals read whole): Harappa mound 18 of 31 against 14.84 shuffled (P 0.030); building 0 of 10, trench 11 of 31 against 9.85 (P 0.35). So at Harappa the manager sits in the right mound, not the right building. Rescue 2 (new idea): the Harappa buildings are grid squares, far smaller than a Mohenjo-daro house, so building-level cannot hold the effect; the next test is neighbouring grid squares (a square and its eight neighbours). The Mohenjo-daro result stands where the building is a house.

**Lead 1 (c), Harappa size.** 8 managers, median 524 mm2 against 581 for 245 other seals (P 0.59): not bigger there. Only 8 managers; stands as Mohenjo-daro plus pooled result.

**Lead 1 (d), the manager does not copy the master's stand letter or animal.** Against master-crest seals in the same building: stand letter matches 1 of 5 pairs (shuffle 1.35, P 0.78); species 5 of 5 (shuffle 3.25, P 0.18). Same area: stand 4 of 32 against 8.75 (P 0.98, fewer than chance), species 40 of 63 against 40.9. Same city: stand 38 of 139 against 39.8, species 184 of 291 against 190.6. Rescue 1 (redesign, household as a whole): manager against all other seals in the same building: stand 16 of 70 against 19.2, species 83 of 124 against 80.9; same area: stand 442 of 1,628 against 456. Rescue 2 (new idea): the manager's own stand mix (result 4 above): that is the real signal, a manager takes S or J. Pairs of managers of one master name: 2 pairs share a letter, 0 differ (too few). The "manager shares the household mark" reading is not supported; the "manager is a mark class of his own" reading is.

**Lead 2 (a), one office-holder per quarter.** Different holders of 142 do NOT sit in different areas more than chance. Mohenjo-daro, 21 objects in 7 areas, holder key with office words skipped: pairs with different holders in the same area 35 against 35.0 shuffled (P 0.71); same holder in different areas 5 against 5.0; with the registry crest 34 against 31.9 and 22 against 19.9. Blocks: 16 objects in 14 blocks, cross-holder pairs 2 against 1.9. Harappa 12 objects, 9 with an area: 1 cross pair against 0.7. Rescue 1 (redesign, location only): the 142 objects sit in 14 different blocks against 13.6 for random draws of 16 named objects from the city (P 0.54), 7 areas against 8.9 (clustered if anything, P 0.16). Rescue 2 (new idea, the sign next to 142 is the office word, not the family): the sign after 142 is 617 in 29 of 112 uses and 615 in 25; 617 holds three Mohenjo-daro objects across early, middle and late bins (M-1976, M-382, M-1335 `142-617` / `817-002-142-617`), so the "holder" key is partly an office word, which is why the holder statistic is flat. Result: 142 is not a one-per-quarter office; it is spread at random like any common title. The numbered form (result 5) is the live version of the quarter idea.

**Lead 2 (b), wards.** The ward signs 741/742/745 stand in 1 of the 11 pair texts (M-837, `575-125-741-031-061-142-740`: 741 then count 031 then quarter then 142) against 11 of 379 (3%) for other quarter texts (P 0.29). No excess. A count after 142: 0 of 11.

**Lead 2 (c), the office staying with a house over time.** 25 of the 38 objects with 142 have a time bin (16 middle, 7 late, 2 early). Same holder in different bins: 5 pairs against 6.1 shuffled (P 0.68); with the registry crest 17 against 14.3 (P 0.10). The one clean holder is the comb 400 at Harappa (5 objects, all middle), so no cross-time test is possible there. Not tested further: needs early objects; there are 2.

**Lead 2 (d), other signs that behave like 142.** The first design (crest labels shuffled, different crests in the same area) flagged 32 of 148 signs at P 0.05 (chance 7.4) but the list is the jar 740, the comb 400, the man 090, 176, 920, 741: signs of houses and endings, because common signs inherit house clustering. 142 itself does not show it (P 0.78). Redesign (location only, Mohenjo-daro blocks, 84 signs with 8+ located objects): spread P at or below 0.05 for 3 signs (chance 4.2): 255 (32 objects in 29 blocks against 23.8, P 0.012), 002 (P 0.032), 520 (P 0.048); none at 0.01 beyond 255. Clustering at or below 0.05: 806, 415 (chance level). So no other sign shows "one holder per quarter". The signs with 142's text position (mean relative position 0.49) and many holders: 690, 615, 255, 923, 590, 706, 368, 240 behave as name signs with house clustering, not as offices. Other offices by ending are already listed in `registry.md` (chief 520, keeper 151-161, comb 400, guards 525-527, 368, 842); no new one came out of this.

**Lead 3 (a), the prediction that name+amount is more than half.** It is 44% of the 816 readable records (356), the biggest kind but not a majority; amount+amount as a plain two-face kind is not among the 12 commonest kinds (the two-amount tablet shows up as the three-face E11 tablet, amount+amount+thing 24).

**Lead 3 (b), size of the batch evidence.** Only 3 batches reach 5 copies, and the two in FTrench I are one pair; the pair test with batches of 5+ or 3+ has too few pairs to test (1 to 4 signatures per trench). The result in 8 above rests on all signatures.

**Lead 3 (c), amount versus depth.** The name 176-740-400 (27 records with a depth, 9.0 to 19.5 ft): cup count against depth Spearman -0.18 (shuffle P 0.36); 3-cup records median 13.0 ft, 4-cup median 13.3 ft, 2-cup 15.8 ft. No drift through depth. The amounts coexist at one depth; an account running over time is not shown by depth. Other names with several amounts have 0 to 3 depth-dated records. Rescue (new idea): the amount might follow the physical format, but 176-740-400 is incised on 28 of its 31 records; format is not it. Open: month or season, not recorded in the catalogue.

**Lead 3 (d), breaks.** Two cup counts in the whole catalogue (not in the 902 records) break the rule: object 434 Harappa `001-034-700` (a short-stroke 1 beside a stacked 4 in front of the cup, read as 5), and object 735 Harappa `000-017-700-400` (stack 7, with a lost sign in front). The first mixes two stroke series in one count run, so the sum 5 is a reading of the run and not a written 5; the second is a count of 7 on a damaged face. Both listed as the only possible breaks.
