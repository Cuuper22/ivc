# Beads, cotton, and sign 838

Scripts (in `work_scripts/`, run `python3` from there): `beads_1_sites.py` (signs and sign pairs at the bead towns and the Harappa Mound E workshop dump), `beads_2_scan.py` (one feature table for 202 signs, ranks bead and cloth candidates), `beads_3_cotton.py` (the cotton checks), `beads_4_foreign.py` (signs on seals found abroad crossed with the bead towns), `beads_5_838.py` (the 838 split). Tables in `work_scripts/beads_out/` (`scan.csv`, `foreign.csv`, `site_*.csv`, `pairs_CL.csv`). Every script has its predictions written at the top, before the numbers. Reading order = stored order reversed. A "face" is one text on one object side; "x" means times chance; P is a one-sided Fisher test. `measured` = a number out of the scripts, `looked up` = from a named page fetched today, `guess` = mine. Dictionary words come from the local copy of the Dravidian etymological dictionary (DEDR), Tamil side.

## What was found

1. **Sign 390 (a tree with branches at the top) is cotton: the cotton plant, Tamil *parutti*, "Indian cotton plant, Gossypium herbaceum; cotton" (DEDR 3976); the cloth word *pañci* (3836) is the fall-back sound.** `guess` for the sound, `measured` for the reasons:
   - Five terracotta objects from Karanpura (Rajasthan; ids 276.1, 1757.1, 5568.1, 5569.1, 5570.1) carry 390 and nothing else. `looked up`: the Karanpura excavation pages on harappa.com say the inscribed objects there are spindle whorls "used by weavers". A spindle whorl is the little clay disc that weights a hand spindle. Five whorls out of five from the site carry the same one sign. Among the 28 terracotta "misc" objects in the whole catalogue, 5 are 390-alone (3.6 times chance, P 0.011).
   - 390 is a counted thing in lots: 86 of its 90 counted uses (96%) have a count of 3 or more in front, against a median of 33% for the 35 other counted nouns (only 3 nouns are higher: 585, 845, 923, which are rare or copper-list signs; 405, a tree variant, is next at 92%). Counts in front of 390 are 3 (29 times), 4 (23), 5, 6 (tall strokes, 14), 7 and 8, and almost never 1 or 2. Bales and hanks are counted in lots.
   - It goes abroad: 5 of the 36 faces from Ur, Susa, Kish, Bahrain, Failaka, Oman and Central Asia carry it (2.8 times chance, P 0.03). Ur III texts list Meluhhan weavers (`travelled_name.md`).
   - The box (590) + 390 pair, "chest of cotton", sits in the Harappa granary store area 4.9 times chance (7 of 112 faces, P 0.0005). Box 590 alone was already 3.8 times chance there.
   - It sits where craft towns are: Chanhu-daro 2.5 times (9 of 74 faces, P 0.01); the Mohenjo-daro dyers' quarter (the VS area where the dye-trough room was dug) 2.0 times (10 of 101 faces, P 0.026).
   - What it opens: the "box + tree" pair on 71 objects in 21 Mohenjo-daro houses, in 9 cities and abroad, which was read "Chest-guard" (a keeper of stored goods), becomes a **cotton-bale house**: *peṭṭi parutti*, "chest of cotton". The nine-city name with a branch in Mesopotamia and one in Central Asia is a cloth-trading house, the same kind of firm the Ur III weaver lists describe. The 390 signs in the closing slot of seals (39% of its uses are last in the text) read as a trade title, "the cotton man", after the name.
   - The tree is one drawing with four numbers: 390, 392, 405, 407 are all the same picture (P086 in the Mahadevan list). 405 is counted in lots too (92% with count 3 or more, 26 uses) and 407 is the "tree lot" on 63 copper tablets. They are the next cloth or fibre words to test (`guess`: 405 yarn, 407 bolts of cloth listed on copper tablets).

2. **Sign 838 is sesame (*eḷ*, DEDR 854), not bone.** `measured` splitter: the jar-dot sign 741 stands straight before 838 in 3 of its 19 faces (M-2316, M-2403, M-2825; chance rate per slot 1.0%, P 0.0009): "jar of 838", a packed good. Nobody packs bone in a jar; sesame oil goes in jars. Further numbers:
   - A material label would tell the material. The bone rod M-2394 and the ivory rod M-3841 carry the identical text `240 838 585 740 400`: the text does not name the stuff the rod is made of. 1 of the 38 distinct rod texts is shared across two materials, and it is the one with 838.
   - 838 is in the name slot, before the jar, in 17 of 19 faces (it is part of a person's or house's name, a trade word like the fish). It is not on pots (0 of 635) and on tags once (1 of 262), so it is not a contents label either: it is a *dealer's* word, "the sesame man", "of the sesame house".
   - The rods remain 13 times chance even after identical copies are collapsed (2 of 38 distinct rod texts against 15 distinct texts with 838 in the whole catalogue, P 0.0097). Two rod owners carry 838, and they sit in the slot where the other rod house has `013 840` ("three pairs"): 240 is the shared house crest. Why sesame dealers own ivory rods is open; the bone reading stays alive for the rods only. `guess`.
   - Whole sign, whole text: Lothal tag L-153 and Chanhu-daro seal C-19 say just "[the] sesame [man]": a name-only stamp. Chanhu-daro has 838 8 times chance (3 faces, P 0.025).

3. **The bead trade has a quantity fingerprint: 055 (twelve strokes) and 048 (seven strokes in brackets).**
   - 055, the dozen: 10.3 times chance on seals found abroad (4 of 36 faces: Bahrain, Susa, Saar, Janabiyah; P 0.0006) and 5.0 times at the two bead towns together (15 of 282 faces at Lothal + Chanhu-daro, P 1.7e-7). Of all 118 signs with 3 or more foreign faces it is the sign that passes both tests with the strongest numbers (`beads_4_foreign.py`; the other two passes are 090, the man sign, and 001, "one"). Beads are the thing sold in dozens of strings across the Gulf. `guess`.
   - 048, a bundle of seven: 10 of the 18 faces are Lothal wooden-box sealings (16.7 times chance at Lothal, P 3e-12), one is the Mohenjo-daro silver seal M-3550 (silver objects: 3 in the catalogue, 1 with 048; chance 1%), three are Harappa faience tablets `851 741 048 740`. The picture, strokes in brackets, is a string with seven beads on it. Sound `guess`: *kōvai* "string, row" (DEDR 2176, from *kō* "to string beads"). Lothal L-161 to L-170 then read "two shares: a string of seven, of the house": ten boxes of strung beads from one house, sealed at the bead port.
   - I did not find a plain bead noun. See the third section.

4. **Dyers' quarter seal.** M-3217 (id 3217.1, Mohenjo-daro VSA, block 1III) `817 002 005 390` sits next to the excavated dyers' workshop (VS area, block I, house II, room 23; `looked up`, harappa.com "dyers workshop or restaurant"). It reads "two shares, five, cotton". The seal found in the dyer's room itself, M-3466 (`000 150`, block 1II room 23), has a broken sign and the unread 150.

## What went against, with the rescue tried

- Cotton check C1 predicted P below 1e-6 for 390-alone on terracotta misc objects and got 0.011: the five whorls are from one site, and Fisher against the whole catalogue is weak with 28 objects. Rescue: compare inside Karanpura, where 5 of 5 carry it against 390 on 4.9% of faces overall (0.049^5 = 3e-7). Standing as a one-site result that needs a second whorl site.
- C2 predicted 390 on ivory rods (spindle shafts). Two rods carry it alone (M-4037 `390`, M-5533 `390 390 390`) but that is 1.0 times chance (P 0.62). Rods are not the cotton object. C3's second half (other single-sign texts being 40% non-seal) was wrong too: 83% of single-sign texts are on non-seal objects, so the single-sign test does not separate 390 from anything.
- The grain-store pair at Harappa (`590 390`) fits dates or timber as well as cotton; the whorls are what a date-palm reading cannot explain. The earlier "timber" reading of 390 gave no object test; this one gave a whorl.
- The cloth-impression check on tag backs still cannot run: the free pages (harappa.com Lothal cretulae paper, Frenez 2017 on Scribd) list impression types only in general ("doors, wooden boxes, pottery jars, cloth/leather bags") and give no object numbers; Frenez's catalogue is closed. Rescue ways tried instead: the whorl find, the count-lot behaviour, the dyers' block, the foreign seals. Price-note for Cúper: the Lothal sealing catalogue entries (Frenez 2020 / Joshi and Parpola 1987) would settle which tags had cloth backs; I did not find a free copy.
- Bead scan (`beads_2_scan.py`): of 202 signs only one scored 4 or more of 6 bead features (060, 0.57 expected by independence); 060 is the very common opener (217 faces) and passes by volume, so it is not read as a bead word. The cloth score gave 5 signs at 4 or more (2.9 expected): 390, 060, 055, 226, 070; only 390 has the object evidence above. Chanhu-daro's own excess was on 031 (a count stroke), the 'alternation' marker and the openers 817/920/060; Lothal's on 048, 500, 055, 002, 000, 001.
- Bead findspots: web searches found the dyers' room (Mohenjo-daro VS area A, block I, house II, room 23) and Harappa Mound E Southwest (faience and bead dump, no trench number). Mound E in the catalogue holds the cup 700 (2.4 times), the four-stroke 034 (3.4 times), 347 deer (9 of 10 faces, all on tablets of the 347-741-176 formula) and 711 (21 faces, all Harappa), none of them a bead word by picture or by object. The Mohenjo-daro bead-shop rooms and the Lothal bead-factory squares were not found in a free source, so those two matches are not run. Two inscribed bead objects exist (Harappa F43 clay bead `700`, Harappa square bead `033`): too few.
- Pictures: sign shapes show one drilled-bead picture, the "garlic" signs 370 to 372 (a vertical stroke broken by a circle with a dot, a bead on a cord). They are 22 faces, mostly the first sign of a seal, 8 of 10 text-initial; no bead object carries them. Left as an opener variant.
- 838 check D1 (parsed name share 89% against 72% for fish, predicted at most 35%) went against: the parser puts most signs in the name slot, so that share does not split sesame from bone. D2 predicted a ratio below 10 once rod copies are collapsed and got 13. The splitter that did work is D4 (741 before 838, P 0.0009) plus D3 (one text on bone and ivory).
- Bead noun: three ways in (workshop and bead-town enrichment; foreign seals crossed with bead towns; material and picture search) gave quantity signs (055, 048) but no noun. Pausing the noun hunt, reason: the bead towns write their goods on tags and sealings, and the one tag-heavy bead town (Lothal) uses a closed small word set (500, 048, 055, 705, 090, 741); 500 and 741 are the unread ones beside the string. Next leads, not run: 500 (11 of 24 faces on tags, Lothal only, opens the dozen tag) and 741 (jar-dot) as the bead container or lot words.
