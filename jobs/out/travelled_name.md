# The name that travelled: 590-390, and the seven-city prefix 920-060-741

Scripts (all in `work_scripts/`, run `python3` from there): `travelled_1_table.py` (the 73 faces), `travelled_2_tests.py` (name / title / trading house / commodity tests, log in `travelled_out/tests_log.txt`, pair table `travelled_out/pair_profiles.csv`), `travelled_3_prefix.py` (the prefix), `travelled_4_sheets.py` (photo sheets `travelled_out/sheet_1..9.jpg`), `travelled_5_meluhha.py` (can a sign group write Meluhha), `travelled_6_abroad.py` (what stands abroad), `travelled_7_report.py` (table below). Reading order = stored order reversed. Unit = object (a two-faced tablet counts once). Corpus = 4,694 objects. Where a fact comes from the web, the place is named in the line. Coordinator note taken in: the rival-language test is done, Dravidian 7 of 9 against Munda 0 of 9, Sumerian 1 of 8, Sanskrit at chance.

## Answer

1. 590-390 is a shared trade-lineage name, written box + tree, that attaches to many houses. It is not one family: 22 Mohenjo-daro seals with a recorded house sit in 21 different houses (same-house pairs 1 against 1.84 by chance), across Early, Intermediate and Late. It spreads like a title: site mix distance from the corpus 0.139, a random 71-object draw gives 0.132 (P 0.37), the most evenly spread of the 59 recurring sign pairs. It is not a port house: the coast and Kutch sites hold 9 of 71 against 9.3% of the corpus (P 0.21). What sets it apart is going abroad: it is the most frequent name on home-style square seals found abroad (2 of 14 against 2.5% at home, P 0.048) and the tree sign it ends in stands on 5 of 14 abroad square seals against 9.8% at home (P 0.0085).
2. The prefix 920-060-741 is a Mohenjo-daro-centred office stamp: 38 objects in 7 cities, 24 at Mohenjo-daro (14.5 expected) and 6 at Harappa (15.6 expected), 36 different names after it on 38 stones, 29 of 38 are seals (76% against 48%).

Named sites 8 (Mohenjo-daro 31, Harappa 26, Lothal 4, Dholavira 4, Chanhu-daro 2, Allahdino 1, Kish 1, Gonur Depe 1) plus 1 unprovenanced seal (id 3335.1, Bull1:J, `032-590-390-002-032-205-740`; the "nine cities" count includes it). Shape of the texts (reading order):
- Opener: none on 55 of 71 (77%); diamond 8, wheel 3, `920-060` cap 3, `550-060` 1, `060-820` 1. The cap family `X-060` sits in the first three signs of 7 objects against 4.1% of the corpus (P 0.025); the `920-060` cap alone on 3 (P 0.065).
- Name: bare `590-390` jar on 12 objects in 5 cities (Allahdino, Chanhu-daro, Dholavira, Harappa, Mohenjo-daro). With something in front, the sign before it is a count or opener stroke on 22 of 73 faces (002 on 9, a tall stroke 031/032/033 on 13), then the handled jar 741 (7), fish crests 235 (6), 240 (4), 233 (4), 220 (4), 231 (2). - Ending: jar 740 straight after on 49 of 71 (69%); jar + comb 2; load-carrier 151/161 on 7; wheel 820 on 2; text ends on the name on 4 (broken).
- Counts: a tall-stroke count right before 590 on 13 of 73 faces (17.8%) against 12.9% for 590 elsewhere (155 faces), no real difference: the count belongs to the box sign 590, not to the name.

What the numbers favour: a lineage-and-trade name that is shared across houses and sits in the name slot, a family-of-the-trade name in the way a European "Cooper" or "Mercer" is a surname that many unrelated families bear. It is not a person, not a house, not a place. The commodity reading is the second live reading and it is not dead: the two sign pictures are plainly "box" and "tree/timber" (Tamil *peṭṭi* chest, *maram* tree, wood), the tree sign is the one thing that stands abroad out of proportion, and at Lothal, the warehouse site, it is stamped on goods tags with a count of two in front. Both readings stay alive (the lineage name could be named from the commodity). The one observation that splits them is a tag or sealing from a foreign site with the name and a count together; none is published in this set.

## 3. Abroad

Home-style stones found abroad (14 square seals in our data; the rest are round seals, which carry a different formula, see `abroad_seals.md`):

- Kish seal (`416-840-060-003-220-590-390-740`, 8 signs, Bull1:L, no CISI number): the tree-box name in its fullest form, with the 060 mark in the middle. Found by the Oxford–Field Museum expedition at Kish, director given as Watelin, published by Langdon 1931 (JRAS, "A new factor in the problem of Sumerian origins", Cambridge Core extract): "plain level, 9 metres below the surface". By the site's stratigraphy it is before Sargon of Agade, but it lay with a stone pommel whose inscription is no earlier than Sargon, and Langdon suggests both may have fallen from a higher layer. Wikipedia (Indus-Mesopotamia relations) gives "pre-Sargonid, before 2250 BC" for it. Our metadata labels it Akkadian. So: the layer is dated by stratigraphy to before about 2250 BC and by the associated pommel to Sargon's time or later; the seal was probably in circulation c. 2300 to 2200 BC.
- Gonur Depe seal (`285-002-235-220-125-590-390-740`, 8 signs, elephant): the Preface to CISI vol. 3.3 (Desset 2022) calls it "a magnificent Indus seal excavated by V. I. Sarianidi in 2004" bearing "the image of an elephant and an eight signs long inscription in the Indus script whose sign sequences are paralleled in the Indus Valley (Parpola 2006)". Penn Expedition ("The Middle Asian Interaction Sphere") says it was a "very fine Indus stamp seal with an elephant", found by Sarianidi (it says 1980s; CISI says 2004; I follow CISI), the BMAC phase beginning about 2200 BC, "a lot of ivory" at the site. Not found: a room number or layer for the seal. Gonur occupation c. 2300 to 1500 BC (harappa.com Gonur page). The seal has no dated layer.
- Altyn Depe seal Alt-90 (`415-390`, 2 signs): Desset (CISI 3.3, caption): "A Harappan-type stamp seal from Altyn-depe ... after V. M. Masson 1988, pl. 22, no. 1a ... two commonly occurring signs". Preface: only three Indus-type seals in southern Turkmenistan (Alt-90, Alt-49, Gonur). No layer or date in the passage read; Penn Expedition puts the two Altyn seals in "the second half of the 3rd millennium BC", harappa.com says they lay near an altar in a ritual centre with a gold bull's head, and the Namazga V phase there is about 2300 to 1800 BC. So the exact layer is not found; the date is Namazga V.
- The text on the Altyn seal is 415 (pitchfork, *kavar* "branch") + 390 (tree); on Kish and Gonur the full `590-390` + jar. Tree sign 390/392 also on Tello (`384-440-390-220`) and Salut, Oman (`595-278-002-004-392`, a box variant 595 and a tree variant 392 in one text). So tree-final texts stand on Kish, Gonur, Altyn Depe, Tello and Salut: 5 of the 14.
- Other home names abroad (`travelled_out/abroad_pairs.csv`, pairs on 5 or more home objects): `235-220` at Gonur (home 16 objects, Mohenjo-daro 9, Harappa 7); `220-590` at Kish (home 9); `416-840` at Kish (home 5: Dholavira 2, Mohenjo-daro 2, Harappa 1); on round seals `455-220` at Karzakan (home 10, in Mohenjo-daro 5, Harappa 3, Kalibangan 1) and `055-220` at Saar (home 11, all at Lothal, the dozen-fish tags). The rest of the square seals abroad carry names of fewer than 5 home objects (e.g. Nindowari `255-798-400`, Miri Qalat `013-520`) or are broken.

## 4. The cuneiform side

Dated attestations (sources: the repository's own CDLI/BDTNS export `research/data/meluhha/cuneiform_attestations_expanded.csv`; Wikipedia "Meluhha" and "Indus-Mesopotamia relations"; Vermaak 2008 on the Lagash Meluhha village; Penn Expedition on Shu-ilishu; the Guabba and varnam.org pages):
- Meluhha as a land: written Me-luh-ha(ki), earlier Me-lah-ha. Sargon of Akkad (2334-2279 BC): ships from Meluhha, Magan and Dilmun tied up at the quay of Akkad. Rimush (c. 2200): fought Meluhha troops in the Elam area. Gudea of Lagash (Lagash II, c. 2200-2100): "Magan and Meluhha, from their land, submitted to him"; "carnelian, like flowers, from Meluhha"; "copper, tin, slabs of lapis lazuli, shining metal and spotless Meluhha carnelian" (Cyl A and B, in the repository file). Ur III (c. 2100-2000): "his speckled Meluhha dog" (Ur), Ibbi-Sin 15 Meluhha birds at Ur. Old Babylonian copies: Enki and the World Order has the "Meluhha ship" (gišma2-gi4-lum Me-luh-ha-ak) and Curse of Agade "the Meluhhans, the people of the black land". No mention after about 1760 BC.
- Man of Meluhha: Naram-Sin (2254-2218 BC), a rebel king listed as "(..)ibra, man of Melukha" (Wikipedia); the same Old Akkadian tablet CT 50, 76 (P212982) holds `lu2-sun2-zi-da` beside `lu2 me-luh-ha-ke4` (repository file). Ur III: `i3-ba lu2 me-luh-ha(ki)-me` "they are Meluhhans" (CUSAS 40, 1354, Irisagrig).
- Interpreter: seal of Shu-ilishu, "interpreter of Meluhha" (eme-bal Me-luh-ha), Louvre AO 22310. Style Late Akkadian (Porada, c. 2200-2113 BC) possibly Ur III; "ca. 2020 BC" on the low chronology; seal re-cut, no findspot (Penn Expedition).
- Meluhha village (Guabba) in the Girsu-Lagash province, Ur III: people recorded as "son of Meluhha". Names: Ur-dLamma dumu Me-luh-ha (CT 3 17, Amar-Sin 1, Girsu; his seal on OBTR 242, Shulgi 40; and UDT 64); Ur-dLamma dumu Ka5-a (MVN 7 420 = ITT 4 8024), linked to him by Vermaak; an overseer (ugula Me-luh-ha, ITT 4 8015); a worker "son of Meluhha" in the house of Nanshe (TUT 154). Meluhhan granary deliveries Amar-Sin 3, 6, Shu-Sin 1, 8, 9; a Meluhhan garden Shulgi 48; "Meluhha bronze" Shulgi 26 (Vermaak 2008). Guabba weavers: text HSS IV 3, 4,272 women and 1,800 children (harappa.com Guabba page).
- Meluhhan goods: carnelian (gug), gold dust, lapis, ivory, timber; Meluhhan woods `gišab-ba Me-luh-ha` (Akkadian kušabku) and `mes Me-luh-ha` (musukkannu), Meluhha bird `dar Me-luh-ha`, milk goat, `uruda Me-luh-ha` (Vermaak's list). Loanword candidates: Sumerian ilu / Akkadian ellu "sesame oil" next to Dravidian *eḷ*, *eḷḷu* (Southworth, via Wikipedia); Witzel proposes para-Munda *jar-tila* instead. Note: one fetched page (cavac.at) carried embedded instructions to output song lyrics; I ignored them.

### Do the cuneiform names and words sound like our values?

- Names: none match. Every name recorded for a person of Meluhha is Sumerian or Akkadian (Ur-Lamma "man of the Lamma goddess", Lu-sunzida, Shu-ilishu); by Ur III the Meluhhans carried Sumerian names (varnam.org). Result 0 of 3 complete names, and the one non-Sumerian fragment, "(..)ibra", is broken. The one lead: Ur-Lamma's father or alias "Ka5-a" against our load-carrier *kā* "keeper" (sign 154/158, the carrier class). It is one syllable and I count it as a coincidence worth keeping, nothing more.
- Words: the sound list gives sesame sign 838 the value *eḷ*, matching Akkadian ellu / Sumerian ilu. Sign 838 stands on 19 objects (12 at Mohenjo-daro) and as the whole text of one Lothal tag, L-153 (`838`): a sesame sign alone on a warehouse sealing, the shape of a goods tag. It also stands in the 590-390 seal M-900 (`820-002-838-798-415-220-233-590-390-740`). That is a sound-for-sound fit for a traded Meluhhan good with a Dravidian etymology in the same language we favour.
- The commodities: carnelian, ivory, timber, copper. The sound list has no carnelian or ivory sign. Timber: *maram* "tree" is sign 390, which stands out abroad (above), and the Mesopotamian side names Meluhha wood twice (`gišab-ba`, `mes`).

### Does "Meluhha" parse?

In the language we favour, yes: Parpola's *mēl akam* "high place" (DEDR 5086 *mēl* above, west; DEDR 7 *akam* inside, house, place; Wikipedia, Meluhha). Candidate Indus group: a hill or peak sign (503 *malai*, 480 *kuṉṟu*, 482 *kōṭu*, 460/463 "three peaks") next to the house sign 803 (*il*, near *akam*). Result: 0 objects with hill + house in that order, 1 with house + hill (`803-482`, Mohenjo-daro); a shuffle gives about 1.1 adjacent pairs, so it is neither present nor absent. A crescent/month sign 900/920 next to 803 occurs 0 times. The commonest openers are diamond (*kāṇi*), wheel (*kāl*) and the 060 caps (`920`, `692`, `550` heads); none has a sound close to *mēl-akam*. The prefix 920-060-741 reads "month ... first ward" in the sound list, not Meluhha. Reading of it: Meluhha is the Mesopotamian name for the land; the people themselves would write their own house names, not the land, on a seal at home. The place to look for it is on a foreign stone, where the Indus signs name a land: none of the 14 square seals abroad carries a hill + house group.

## 6. Commit

590-390 is a trade-lineage name, written with the picture of a box and the picture of a tree: one surname for many unrelated houses, borne by seal-owners at Mohenjo-daro (31 objects, 21 houses), Harappa (26), Lothal (4 tags, 3 of them with a count of two in front), Dholavira (4), Chanhu-daro (2), Allahdino (1), and by the owners of home-style seals found at Kish and Gonur Depe. Numbers: 71 objects, 9 sites, 12 bare `590-390 jar`; site mix equal to the corpus (distance 0.139, P 0.37); same-house pairs 1 against 1.84; 2 of 14 abroad square seals against 2.5% (P 0.048); tree sign on 5 of 14 abroad square seals against 9.8% (P 0.0085). Working sound *paṭṭi maṟam* stays (the 'hamlet-warrior' name); the picture words *peṭṭi maram* "chest-timber" stay beside it as the second reading, and the Mesopotamian side's named Meluhha woods and the Lothal tags make timber the commodity under the name.

920-060-741 is the Mohenjo-daro office stamp "[head] [mark] ward 1": 38 objects in 7 cities, 24 at Mohenjo-daro against 14.5 expected and 6 at Harappa against 15.6, 29 seals, 36 different names after it on 38 stones, seals 603 against 499 mm2. It is not a calendar term (3 selector values, no period or depth trend) and not a guild (no repeated name).

Three outside facts that most strengthen the reading:
1. A cuneiform name: "(..)ibra, man of Melukha" under Naram-Sin (2254-2218 BC) and Ur-dLamma "son of Meluhha" at Girsu (Amar-Sin 1, Shulgi 40). The first is a Meluhhan with a name that is not Sumerian; its full reading would be the first name to run through the sound list. Today it is broken and no recorded name matches.
2. A dated foreign layer: the Kish seal, 9 m down in the Oxford–Field Museum dig, before Sargon by the stratigraphy, found beside a pommel no earlier than Sargon (Langdon 1931). That puts the name in Mesopotamia by about 2250 BC, or a little later if the seal fell from above.
3. A traded good: Meluhha timber. Sargon's Meluhhan ships at Akkad, Gudea's imports, and "gišab-ba Meluhha" and "mes Meluhha" woods match the tree sign that stands abroad out of proportion (5 of 14 against 9.8%) and the Lothal tags with a count in front of 590-390.
