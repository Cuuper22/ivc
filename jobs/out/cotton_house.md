# The cotton house: is 590-390 a keeper or a trade, a second whorl site, and the fibre family

Scripts (in `work_scripts/`, run `python3` from there): `cotton_common.py` (loader), `cotton_1_split.py` and `cotton_1b_more.py` (part 1), `cotton_2_whorls.py` and `cotton_2b_rescue.py` (part 2), `cotton_3_fibre.py` and `cotton_3b_frames.py` (part 3), `cotton_4_read.py` (the readings), `cotton_5_report.py` (this file). Tables and logs in `work_scripts/cotton_out/`. Every script has its predictions written at the top before the numbers. Reading order = stored order reversed. A "face" is one text on one object side. "x" means times chance; P is a one-sided test (small P = unlikely by luck). `measured` = a number out of the scripts, `looked up` = from a page fetched today, `guess` = mine.

Plain words used below. *Name slot*: the place in a seal text just before the closing jar, where the owner's name or house name stands. *Goods slot*: where the thing counted stands (a number comes before it). *Jar* (sign 740): the ending "of the house". *Counted*: a number sign stands right before it. *Whorl*: the small clay disc that weights a hand spindle. *Frame*: the pair (sign on the left, sign on the right) around a sign. *Distinct text*: identical copies (moulded tablets, tags) collapsed into one.

## What was found

1. **590-390 is both: it stands in the name slot, and what it names is a goods word. It is a house named after its trade.** `measured`
   - Name slot: the jar follows the pair directly on 53 of 73 faces (73%). Among the 164 two-sign combinations that occur 10 or more times, the middle one has jar-after of 0.00; 590-390 sits at the 87th percentile. In the long-text phrase table the pair is in 17 of 17 phrases of the who-kind (13 owner phrases, 2 title phrases, 1 name, 1 account line with a name) and in no pure count line. Both readings predicted this, so it does not split them.
   - 390 on its own is a goods word, not a name word. Outside the box pair 390 takes the jar on 4 of 216 places (1.9%) against 53 of 73 inside the pair (P 3e-36). Outside the pair it is counted (a number right before it) on 90 of 216 places (42%), and 96% of those counts are 3 or more (3 appears 30 times, 4 26, 6 15). By how often a number stands before it, 390 ranks 10th of 84 signs with 30 or more places. A keeper word has no use for lots of 3 to 8 and no reason to refuse the jar; a cotton word has both. This is the splitter and it goes to B.
   - The box lifts a goods word into the name slot, and not only 390. 123 of the 209 places where the box 590 stands are followed by a tree-family sign (390 73 times, 405 28, 407 19, 406 2, 392 1), against 2.9% of all sign places being tree-family. A keeper or title sign (carrier, spear, man, comb) follows the box on 2 of 209 places. In the box pair, 405 takes the jar on 25 of 28 (89%) and 407 on 17 of 19 (89%); outside it each takes the jar on 2%. Same signs, goods word alone, name word after the box. Reading A (390 = a keeper) would need three separate keeper words that all follow the box and are all counted in lots when alone; it has no account of that.
   - Box with sesame 838 or grain 413: none. Box then 838 or 413 adjacent: 0 and 0 (same face: 1 against 0.8 expected, and 0 against 2.2).
   - Where it is counted like goods: the Lothal warehouse tags. All 4 pair-bearing tags in the catalogue are Lothal tags (P 0.05 against tags elsewhere), 3 of them have `002-032` right before the box (a count of two short strokes then two tall ones), and `002-032` is on no other Lothal tag (3 of 91). On those three the pair is a counted good with a number in front, L-139 and L-196 end on the pair with no jar, L-198 has one. Elsewhere a count stands before the box on 22 of 73 faces (30%, 86th percentile of bigrams), but 9 of those are `002` and 7 of the 9 are the opener's own two strokes after the diamond or wheel.
   - The reading, head-last: Dravidian puts the describing noun first and the head noun last, so box + cotton reads *peṭṭi-parutti* "chest-cotton", cotton that is boxed or baled, and the house is "the boxed-cotton house". It is not "chest of cotton" (that would be the other order). Sound `guess`; the slot facts are `measured`.
   - What it opens: the same frame with 405 and 407 gives two more house names, box + 405 (28 faces) and box + 407 (19 faces), so 120 faces in all name houses by a boxed fibre good: cotton, 405, 407. The nine-city name with a branch in Mesopotamia (Kish) and one in Central Asia (Gonur Depe) is a boxed-cotton house.

2. **The three tree signs are three words in one slot.** `measured` 390, 405 and 407 never stand on the same object: 0 objects shared against 6.8 expected (390 with 405), 7.4 (390 with 407), and 1 against 3.1 (405 with 407). Yet they share frames (same left and right neighbour in different distinct texts) 18, 14 and 15 times against 4.3, 4.0 and 4.8 for random signs of the same frequency (P 0.009, 0.011, 0.059). Slot-mates that exclude each other are what the members of one list look like (cotton, thread, cloth). Each has its own job:
   - 390 = raw cotton, *parutti* (DEDR 3976). Counted 3 to 8 in lots; the whole text of the Karanpura whorls; in Mohenjo-daro's dyers' quarter it is the counted good: 9 of 102 faces outside the box pair there (2.4x, P 0.012), while the box pair itself is not enriched there (1 of 102, 0.76x). The two uses split by place: the quarter holds the cotton, the name goes everywhere.
   - 407 = cloth, *tuṇi* "cloth" (DEDR 3307). On copper-tablet goods lists 63 of 237 faces (11.9x) and 16 of 97 distinct texts (8.4x, P 2e-11).
   - 405 = thread or skeins, *nūl* "yarn, cotton thread" (3726); the counted unit would be the skein, *cīṭu* (2611). Counted in lots (92% have a count of 3 or more, 26 uses) like cotton.

3. **Two whole formulas read in the dyers' quarter and elsewhere.** `[two shares] [number] cotton`, as in M-1833 `861-002-015-390`, M-803 `817-002-005-390` and M-20 (…`861-002-005-390`), is "two shares: five cotton"; it is the counted-goods use of 390 inside the quarter next to the dyer's room. See section 5.

## What went against, and the rescue tried

- **B's prediction that the count before the box is a tall lot count (3 or more) did not come true.** Counts before the box: 2 on 16 faces, 3 on 5, 1 on 1, so only 23% are 3 or more, against 32% for the box without 390 (not different). The lot counts belong to 390 alone, not to the pair. Rescue: the Lothal tags carry `002-032` before the box on 3 of 4 pair tags and nowhere else on Lothal tags; so the pair is counted as a unit there, in a different counting formula from lots of 390. Standing as: lots of cotton are written on 390 itself; boxes of it are written with the two-and-two formula at the warehouse.
- **No second whorl site (part 2).** The catalogue's bin for clay and stone odds and ends (MISC) holds 71 faces outside Karanpura that are not metal; 390 is on 2 of them (0.57x, P 0.87), 405 on 0, 407 on 1. The 11 objects of conical, spherical, circular or shapeless form that are not seals, tablets or tags and not metal and not from Karanpura: 390 on 1. I looked at the 24 candidates that have photographs (sheet `cotton_out/whorl_sheet1.jpg`). Five look like whorl-shaped objects: H-1011, H-1012, H-1013 (flat discs) and H-1014, M-619 (cones). 390 is on none of them; 407 is on H-1014 (`861-013-016-407`: diamond, three, six, 407). The five Karanpura whorls themselves have no photographs in the folder (the folder's Karanpura pictures `Krs-1` and `Krs-2` are seals).
  - Rescue one, a better test (material instead of catalogue bin): terracotta objects outside Karanpura carry 390 on 5 of 87 faces (1.17x, P 0.43); all clay-like material 0.74x. Against. But terracotta **seals** carry it on 4 of 11 against 154 of 1,798 steatite seals (4.2x, P 0.011): Kalibangan K-39 (`590-390`) and K-60, Nuhato, Nausharo Ns-46. Cheap fired copies of seals at the northern and western edge sites favour 390.
  - Rescue two, a new idea (the same single-sign whole-text habit at another place): sign 390 is the whole text of 11 faces in the catalogue: 5 are the Karanpura whorls, 3 are plain pots at Kalibangan, 1 is a Harappa pot, 1 a Harappa object catalogued MISC of shapeless form (H-1515, id 4141.1, no photograph), 1 an ivory rod. Kalibangan (also Rajasthan, like Karanpura) has 3 of its 90 pots carrying 390 alone against 1 of 545 pots elsewhere (5.3x, P 0.0099). So the "whole text is 390" habit does repeat in the region, on pots at Kalibangan, but a second site of whorls does not appear in this catalogue.
  - Web, `looked up`: the harappa.com Karanpura excavation page (preliminary results) says the dig "showed evidence of spindle whorls with graffiti marks (similar to the earliest Indus writing found at Harappa)", caption "from the same floor level of an early Harappan house"; no count of whorls and no sign numbers. The harappa.com video page says the inscriptions "likely belonged to spindle whorls used by weavers". A Swaveda article names spindle whorls at Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Lothal and Dholavira as plain, without writing. I did not find another reported inscribed whorl or textile tool from an Indus site. The Kenoyer and Meadow paper on inscribed objects from Harappa (Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions vol. 3) is the one that could list them; its PDF is linked from harappa.com but I did not open it. Part 2 therefore ends with one site of whorls, a regional echo on pots, and the Karanpura marks being early Harappan graffiti, not the later seal script.
- **Thread (405) failed two of four predictions.** True: counted in lots (92%); not enriched on copper tablets (0.83x). False: it is not at craft places (0 of 102 in the dyers' quarter, 0.66x at Chanhu-daro) and it does not share objects with 390 (0 against 6.8). Its 1.94x on two-sided tablets (44 of 1,108) is copies: 29 of the 44 faces are one moulded tablet `501-405-002-240-520`; on distinct texts it is 0.70x. Rescue one (better test): count the signs it stands beside. It follows sign 501 ("heap, stock") on 29 faces, but that is the one moulded tablet. Rescue two (new idea): the three signs are slot-mates that never meet, so the right test for a thread word is not co-presence with cotton but sharing the box's frame: 405 shares frames with 390 18 times against 4.3. 405 stands: it is a counted fibre-family word in the box name; the craft-town signal belongs to 390, and I have no independent proof it is thread rather than, say, a second kind of cotton (a different grade or place). Building on `nūl`, testing.
- **Cloth (407) failed two of its predictions.** Abroad it is 1.24x (1 of 36), not the 1.5x predicted; and the 3.07x in the dyers' quarter on faces is copies (2 of 73 distinct texts, 1.39x, P 0.43). The copper-tablet signal survives collapsing copies (8.4x). So "cloth" rests on the goods-list slot and the box frame, and the dyers' quarter does not support it. Rescue: the order test (cotton before thread before cloth when two share a face) could not run, because they never share a face; the slot-mate result stands in for it.
- **Dye words: no dye sign found.** I scanned the 32 signs with 4 or more faces in the dyers' quarter. Top at distinct-text level: 745 (the three-dot jar, "third ward") 8.0x, 5 of 30 distinct texts, P 0.0003; the hill sign 503 4.3x, 4 of 45, P 0.014; 350 4.2x. These are ward, hill and name signs, not dye words, and no dye word (*cāyam* 2457 dye, *kāvi* 1490 red ochre, *aviri* 269 indigo, *tōmpu* 3553 red dye) has a sign picked for it. Open. One thing to run next: 503 stands before 615 "field" in 4 quarter texts (`503-615-740`, `503-615-752-740`), "hill-field"; whether that is a dye-plant field is untested.
- Reading A is not dead: "chest" + a keeper word is a head-last compound,. But its two specific predictions (390 is not counted in lots elsewhere; box + other keepers) both failed. A would need 390 to be a keeper only inside the box and a cotton-like counted word everywhere else, a homophone.
- The old sound-list value for 405 and 407 is *kiḷai* "kindred, branch" (DEDR 2018), which passed a same-slot test. That test measures the same thing as the frame result here (slot-mates), so both words stay alive: *kiḷai* "branch/kin" for 405 and 407 if they are name parts, *nūl* and *tuṇi* if they are goods words. The frame result (they stand in the goods slot with a count in front outside the box) favours the goods words.
