# The carrier phrase: [A-variant 465-475] [806] [154/158]

Scripts: `work_scripts/carrier_load.py`, `carrier_analysis.py`, `carrier_tab.py`, `carrier_sheets.py`, `carrier_crop.py`. Reading order = reverse of stored order; photos show stored order left to right (the seal is read right to left, so the phrase sits at the LEFT end of a seal photo and ends the text).

## Boldest reading (building on it)

1. The eleven catalogue "variants" are ONE sign: an A (tent) with a small comb of 3-8 tick strokes hanging at its right foot (bare A when the tablet scribe left the ticks out). The catalogue numbers do not count ticks: 467 and 468 both show 4; 468 shows 4 to 5; 469 (H-143) shows about 7; 473 (M-470) shows 3. Not a counting series; not a few shapes. One sign, drawn per medium and hand.
2. The A-comb sits in the numeral slot of the leaf-on-tree 806. Before 806 the stored corpus has: 002 two strokes (17), A-variants (34), 001 (3), 000 lost (7), text start (53). And the sign after 806 depends on what is in that slot: after 002 comes a fish (240 x5, 235 x3, 231 x3, 236 x2); after A or lost sign comes a load-carrier (154, 158, 155) every time (36 of 36). The slot is `[quantifier] 806 [modifier]`, the A-comb is a quantifier the way 002 is, and it selects the carrier.
3. Reading: `A-comb 806 carrier` = "n (A-units) of leaf-tree goods, carried / by porter": a counted load noun phrase. It is a consignment tag, not a title with a grade. The grade guess is replaced by a quantity guess: the comb ticks are a tally (bare A on tablets = unticked or "one").
4. On longer seals the phrase is a closing counted item: the sign right before it is a numeral on 7 of 12 prefixes (001 five times: H-27, H-143, H-158, M-1683, M-1334; 013 twice: H-154, M-1957), and before that 740 jar three times (H-27, H-143, H-158 are `... 740 jar, 001 one, A-806-154`). Reads "[owner/name] of-jar, one, [A-806-carrier]".
5. Sister frames: carrier 156 takes the numeral 003 (36 of 99 occurrences) and 151 takes 740 (11 of 91) and 097/390; 154 and 158 take 806 (17 of 43, 16 of 37). So load-carrier signs each have a preferred partner: 156 = three-strokes porter, 154/158 = leaf-tree porter, 151 = jar porter. 155: 3 of 7 after 806.

Predictions: (a) more 806-carrier texts will turn up with 002 or 001 in place of A only if the carrier is 156/151: none with 154/158 (0 of 36 so far). (b) a photo of any A on a seal will show a comb; on a clay tablet it may be missing. (c) the unreadable (000) slots H-725, H-727, H-1808, H-1731, M-1324 are lost A; H-725/H-727 sit between H-724 and H-726 (465) in the same number run, so the lost sign is 465. (d) tablets with comb-A carry the end comb 400; tablets with bare A do not (see below).

## 2. Other faces

Only 3 phrase objects have a second row in the catalogue:
- H-160 (seal, 468, 400-154-806-468 on face 1): face 2 `090-017` = man sign plus seven strokes (the photos H-160 C bis and c show two rows of strokes, about 7 above and 5 below the hole). A count, and the other side of this seal is a person-plus-seven.
- M-470 (tablet, 473) and M-1419 (tablet, 467): back face `540` on both = bowtie / X. Photos confirm: M-470 B and M-1419 B show a big X; H-208 B (472) also shows a big X. So 3 of the 7 tablets with an end comb carry the bowtie X on the back (M-470, M-1419, H-208). Other tablet backs photographed (H-189 B, H-190 B, H-724 B / B bis / B ter, H-726 B, H-775 B) are blank or worn.
- No cup 700 plus strokes on any phrase tablet. Counts appear on the one seal back (H-160: 090-017).

Tablet split (14 phrase tablets): bare A 465, no 400 tail: 5 (H-190, H-724, H-726, H-1104, H-1105); comb-A with `400` comb at the end of the text: 7 (H-1999, H-1980, H-189, H-208, M-470, M-1419, H-775); other 2 (H-1788 `470-002-861`, H-1979 ends in 740). Bare A with no 400 against ticked A with 400: 0 of 5 against 7 of 9, Fisher p = 0.02. The `806 235 590 405 740 400` tablets (H-750 to H-754, H-1114, H-472, H-204) also end in 400, so 400 closes the Harappa tablet class.

## 5. 806 before and after (127 occurrences, reading order)

Before 806: text start 53 (all followed by 845 15, 235 14, 233 6, 368 2, 798 2, 740 2, 032 2, 706 2); 002 17; A-variants 34 (465 5, 467 8, 468 9, 472 5, 470 2, 469, 471, 473, 474, 475 once each); lost 000 7; 001 3; 060 2; 231 2; 742 2; others once.
After 806: 235 18, 154 17, 158 16, 845 15, 233 8, 240 7, 032 4, 415 4, 231 4, 798 4, 003 3, 740 3, 155 3, then single cases.
Every carrier-after-806 case (36) has an A or a lost sign before it; the carrier 154/158 never follows 806 after 002 or a text start.
Pairs: 845 begins the mass-produced Mohenjo-daro triangular tablets `806 845 061 407 850 900 740` (about 16 copies, M-582 to M-604, M-1535 to M-2054); 235 begins the Harappa tablets `806 235 590 405 740 400` (8 copies: H-750-754, H-1114, M-472, H-204) and `806 235 240 ...` seals. So tablets in the 806 family come in three set texts: `806 845 ...` (Mohenjo), `806 235 ... 400` (Harappa and Mohenjo), `A 806 158 (400)` (Harappa). Seals: `806 X` unticked starts, `002 806 fish`, `A 806 154`.
Sister phrases with other carrier signs: 156 follows 003 (36 of 99), 435 (12), 031 (10); 151 follows 740 (11 of 91), 097 (8), 390 (7); the pairing 003 + 156 matches 806 + carrier with A as numeral-ish. The 845-806 and 235-806 end texts of the stored order are the text-start 806 texts above.
