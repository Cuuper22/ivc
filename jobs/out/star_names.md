# Star names: numbers inside names

Reading order = stored order reversed. Scripts in `work_scripts/` (run from that folder): `stars_common.py` (finds every number written right before a sign, and its slot), `stars_1_split.py` (step 1 and 2 as first written), `stars_2_forms.py` (rescue tests), `stars_3_compounds.py` (step 3), `stars_4_calendar.py` (step 4, the compound table), `stars_5_rooms.py` (room test redesigned), `stars_6_read.py` (seals read whole), `stars_7_nakshatra.py` (one rescue idea). Tables and printouts: `work_data/stars/` (`units_L.csv`, `units_Mu.csv`, `fish_compounds.csv`, `name_compounds.csv`, `star_seal_readings.csv`, `step*_print.txt`). Readings 81 to 100 are appended to `READINGS.md` under "Star names".

Plain words used below:
- **Number unit**: a run of stroke signs written right before another sign. Three stroke styles: **short** (half-height strokes in one row, signs 001-007), **stacked** (short strokes in two rows, 012-018), **tall** (full-height strokes, 031-036). When two styles stand side by side (`002-032-220`), only the run touching the next sign counts as its number; the earlier run is a separate word. Strokes that the parser gives to the opening formula (diamond or wheel + two strokes) are left out.
- **Slot**, from the template parser (`gloss2.parse_face`): **name** = on a seal, inside the run of name signs that ends in the jar (*kuṭi* "house of") or a closing sign (spear, man, comb, porter), or a seal that is only a name. **Count** = on a goods object (miniature tablet, copper tablet, tag, pot, tool, rod, bangle). On goods objects, "count, named" means the run still ends in a jar or closer; "count, pure" means it does not.
- **Form** = stroke style + value, e.g. "tall2" = two tall strokes.
- **First catalogue** = Lipi. **New texts** = the 1,183 second-catalogue texts with no partner in the first, never used to choose anything. "Distinct texts" = each different text counted once, so 30 moulded copies of one tablet count once.
- **P** = how often chance alone gives a result at least this strong. Small P = the result is real and points the way stated.
- **DEDR** = Burrow and Emeneau's Dravidian Etymological Dictionary (copy in `work_data/lexicons/dedr/`). **Tamil Lexicon** = University of Madras Tamil Lexicon, read on the DSAL site 2026-11-17 (page numbers given).

## 1. What was found

1. **Inside names the number before a fish is spelled, not counted: its stroke form is locked to the fish.** Tall two goes with the plain fish (and the caged fish 226) 65 times out of 73; short two goes with the marked crest fish (231, 233, 235, 236, 240, 241) 62 times out of 63. P 1.6e-28 (first catalogue, distinct name-slot texts). On new texts the same split comes back: 14 of 16 and 17 of 17, P 1.5e-7. In count slots the same split is much looser (tall 9 / short 5 against tall 1 / short 9) and on new texts it does not reach P 0.1. A count would let the quantity choose the strokes; here the word chooses them. This points to number + fish being one word, and it is the first measured difference between a number in a name and a number on an account.
2. **The name slot uses one fixed number per fish; count slots do not.** The commonest form for each fish covers 126 of 186 name-slot uses (68%) against 17 of 43 in count slots (40%); new texts 27 of 38 (71%) against 3 of 9 (33%). Across all signs that take a number, the same comparison is 58% against 49% (first catalogue) and 63% against 52% (new texts): names hold fixed number + sign compounds, accounts vary their numbers.
3. **Room-mates share the number word before a fish.** Seals from the same Mohenjo-daro room, within 3 ft of depth, carry the same number before a fish (whatever the fish drawing) in 5 pairs against 0.74 by chance, P 0.0014 (6.8 times chance; 88 seals carry a number + fish). Counting the plain and caged fish as one star word: 3 pairs against 0.19, P 0.0016. The pairs: M-57 / M-130 and M-367 / M-723 (tall two + caged fish beside tall two + plain fish, each pair in one room), M-240 / M-1044 (tall two + plain fish, room 37 of HR-B 5XXIX), M-266 / M-294 (three + barred fish beside three + plain fish, HR-B 1VIII), M-319 / M-833 (two + fish). This test was redesigned after the exact-compound test had shown those pairs, so it is a test tuned on what was seen; the exact-compound version alone gives 1 pair against 0.15 (P 0.14). The star word belongs to the room, the way the crest fish does (any fish sign: 12 room pairs against 6.53, P 0.03).
4. **Six + fish is a name everywhere it occurs.** All 15 first-catalogue texts and both new texts with six + fish: 13 of 15 and 2 of 2 have the unit alone (H-1028 seal, H-1086 pot) or followed by a closer (spear, jar, jar + man, porter, comb), on seals and on tablets alike. The two others are C-40 (a copper tool, the unit opens a longer list) and H-98 (an unclear sign follows). It is never written as an amount of fish followed by a goods word. The six is written stacked in 13 of 15. This is *aṟu-mīn*, the Pleiades (Tamil Lexicon p. 181: "Pleiades, as containing six stars"; Parpola proposed the reading, credited here; the measured behaviour across object types is new). Its seals take the "manager" ending jar + man in 3 of 10 against 2 of 58 for the tall-two star (compound against ending: Cramer V 0.31 against 0.25 by chance, P 0.025).
5. **The attested Old Tamil star words with a number all occur, each in the name slot:** *eḻu-mīn* "Great Bear" (Tamil Lexicon p. 544, Naṟṟiṇai 231) as the whole of seal H-9 (`017-220`); *mum-mīn* "Mṛgaśiras" (p. 3272) as three + fish on 21 objects (14 in names, 5 on tablets with an owner closer), in Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Kalibangan and Kish in Mesopotamia; *aṟu-mīn* on 17 objects in 4 cities; *nāṇ-mīn* "the lunar mansion, an asterism" (p. 2212, Kalittokai 104; *nāl* "four" DEDR 3655 punning *nāḷ* "day, lunar mansion" DEDR 3656) as four + fish on 7 objects, 5 of them in seal names (B-3, M-64, M-75, M-260, M-1206); the other 2 are counts (H-1192 `004-220-700` four fish then the cup; L-143 tag). Four is the number-as-sound pun that came through.
6. **The commonest star name is tall two + plain fish, on 80 objects (67 first catalogue, 13 new) in 8 cities.** Read *iru-mīn*: *iru* "two" (DEDR 474) punning *iru* "black" (DEDR 2552), the "Dark-star", built like the attested *maimmīṉ* "Saturn, the black star" (Tamil Lexicon p. 3369, Puṟanāṉūṟu 117). A second reading stays alive: *veṇmīṉ* "Venus" (Tamil Lexicon p. 3782, Paṭṭiṉappālai 14), Parpola's reading of two long strokes + fish. The split between them: tall two stands before many other name signs too (box 590, 632, 877, 384, 850, 790, 125, 070), which fits a general word *iru* better than a word kept for Venus.
7. **Twenty seals read whole with star names** (READINGS 81-100), among them H-9 "the Great Bear", H-1028 and pot H-1086 "the Pleiades", Ns-5 "manager of the Pleiades house", M-1912 "Three-star chief", M-260 "manager of the Asterism house", the Kish seal "of the Three-star chest house", and new text 6211 from Chanhu-daro. 69 distinct seal texts carrying a star name now read with every sign given a sound (`work_data/stars/star_seal_readings.csv`, 147 seal texts carry one).

What it opens: names carry star words, so the name slot holds a sky vocabulary. The next tests: (a) the short two before the crest fish as its own word (it also stands before 803 *il* 14 of 18, 806 13 of 16, 798 8 of 9 in names, so it is a name prefix, not a star number); (b) stacked seven before the jar on new texts (6 of 11 name uses) and before 585/575 (14 of 15, 11 of 13): the "Seven" as a second sky or clan word; (c) Venus against Saturn for *iru-mīn*.

Calendar or clan set: the star words that recur are the Dark-star, Mṛgaśiras, the Pleiades, the lunar mansion, the Lone-star, the Great Bear and the two-plus-crest words. That is a short set of named sky objects (7 number words on the plain fish, 5 crest fish each with "two"), not 27 mansions: a clan or house set, read as house names (they sit before the jar *kuṭi* "house of" on 94 of 160 seals carrying one of the 11 compounds found on 4+ seals (jar 81, jar + man 13)).

Animals: 133 of 198 seals with a star name carry the unicorn (67%) against 56% of all seals (P 0.0007); seals with any fish sign 62%. The star names do not split among animals (compound against animal: Cramer V 0.29 against 0.26 by chance, P 0.12): the unicorn is the star houses' animal as a whole.

## 3. What went against, and the rescues

1. **The 3-6-7 pile-up went against.** Name-slot numbers before a fish: 2 136, 1 19, 3 18, 6 10, 4 5, 5 2, 7 1 (191 distinct first-catalogue texts). Share of 3, 6, 7: names 29 of 191 (15%), counts 17 of 47 (36%), P 0.999 the wrong way. New texts: 4 of 43 (9%) against 2 of 13 (15%). Names avoid 1, 4, 5, 8 no more than counts do (14% against 11%). The numbers in names pile up on TWO.
2. **Stroke style as such does not split name from count.** Stacked: names 14 of 191, counts 4 of 47 (P 0.73); tall: 82 of 191 against 19 of 47. What splits them is the form locked to the fish (finding 1), not the style.
3. Rescue 1 (redesigned test, came true): the value mix of name-slot numbers sits further from ordinary goods counts than count-slot numbers do, by 0.14 (P 0.029, slot labels shuffled 5,000 times); new texts +0.15 (P 0.15, same direction). Plus the form lock and fixed-form tests (findings 1 and 2).
4. Rescue 2 (new idea, went against): the name numbers follow the star counts of the 28 lunar mansions (two-star mansions are the commonest numbered kind). Distance to the mansion star counts (Wikipedia table, values 2 and up): name slot 0.35, goods counts 0.14. The goods counts are closer. Paused for the mansion form; the "two" stays read as a word (*iru*), not a star count.
5. Ligatures (went against): 231 (fish with one stroke inside) does not share frames with one + fish written out (0 against median 1); 226 (fish with four strokes round it) does not share frames with four + fish (1 against 1). The room pairs say 226 and 220 are one star word with the tall two; the four strokes round 226 are not a written "four".
6. House sharing of exact compounds went against (tall two + plain fish: 1 pair against 2.62; others 0). The room test (finding 3) carried it at room level only, after redesign.
7. Eight as *eṇ*: all 6 first-catalogue eights and the 1 new-text eight sit in a name run or before a closer (against 60-76% for 3 to 5), but 7 cases is too few to stand on; next signs 336, 390, 596, 405. Under test. One as *or* "unique": one-stroke units in names go before fish, 240, 760, 061 and 350; in pure counts before 142, 821, 156: different neighbours, not yet tested against chance.
