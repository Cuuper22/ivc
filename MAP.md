# STANDING NOTE (2026-11-17, after the decoy tests)
Two blind decoy tests (`jobs/out/decoy_test.md`) show that the prediction procedure used in the sound passes passes a WRONG word of the same job as often as the accepted word (blind: 9 of 58 against 10 of 60; full method with one redesign: 17 of 29 against 19 of 30). So below, wherever a line gives a Dravidian word for a sign and says a prediction "came true" or labels it strict / wide: the JOB of the sign (opener, crest, title, measure, counted thing, place word) is supported; the exact WORD is not. Counts of faces "read whole" mean "a word on every sign". Lines that need no word (template, name structure, findspots, counts, measures in twelves, unit order, time) stand as written. Any new pass on words must mix in decoys and report real against decoy.

## Beliefs

- `measured` Seals found in the same room at Mohenjo-daro, within 3 ft of depth, share an uncommon sign in the middle of the text about 7 times as often as chance (5 pairs against 0.73). First and last signs show no excess.
- `guess` A seal text has three parts: a citywide opening, a middle that belongs to the people of one room (a name or family element), a citywide closing.
- `measured` Among seals that close with the jar sign (Lipi 740), house-mates share the sign right before the jar in 15 pairs against 9.3 by chance, often a rare one, and sometimes the whole last three signs.
- `guess` The sign or pair right before the closing jar is a house or family name; the jar is the "of / belonging to" ending.
- `measured` House-mates share the animal on the seal a little more often than chance (12.5% against 11.0%).
- `guess` The animal marks a larger group (clan, guild, office); the middle of the text marks the household or person inside it.
- `measured` On two-sided tablets one face is the cup sign plus 2, 3 or 4 long strokes (107 / 157 / 123 objects); cup plus 1 stroke occurs 4 times. Tablet area falls about 8% per extra stroke within one tablet format.
- `guess` Cup + strokes is a quantity field (a share, grade or measure) and the other face says who or what it is for.
- `measured` 12 of 17 round seals found in the Gulf, Mesopotamia and Iran carry the man sign or the twin-men sign (Lipi 090 / 091); these signs are rare at home.
- `guess` The round seals abroad write foreign names with Indus signs, and the man sign there is a name marker.

- `measured` Lipi sign numbers are Wells numbers, so every sign now has a described shape (`work_data/sign_shapes.csv`). Short strokes run 1 to 8 and full-height strokes 1 to 5 as two separate series.
- `guess` Two stroke heights are two counting systems for two kinds of thing.
- `measured` After `person-with-two-loads (154/158)` + `leaf-on-tree (806)` the next stored sign is one of 11 A-shaped variants (465-475): 23 of 23 on held-back Harappa texts, 6 of 6 at other sites.
- `measured` The A-shape is one sign with a comb of about 3 to 8 ticks at its foot, sitting in the count slot before leaf-on-tree (806); the load-carrier follows only then (36 of 36). Harappa's granary area holds 11 of 23 of these objects.
- `guess` READING: `[A with n ticks] [leaf-on-tree] [load-carrier]` = "n loads of crop, carried" : a counted load tag. Leaf-on-tree (806) is a crop or produce word; the A-comb is a load count; the carrier sign is the porter or the load. H-152, H-670, H-681, M-1986 read whole this way.

- `measured` Seal texts end in one of a handful of closing signs (jar, spear, plain person, comb, load-carrier). The mix of closers is the same at every site. Seal size goes with the closer: person 738 mm2, jar 645, spear 633, carrier 495, comb 420 (median face area).
- `guess` The closing sign is the owner's class or office, the same system across the whole civilisation, and seal size follows rank. The slot before it is the house name. A seal reads: [opener] [who] [house] [class].

- `measured` On photographs of house-mate pairs the shared sign is on both stones in 24 pairs and cut by the same hand in only 4. The rival reading (one carver repeating his own shapes) is out: different hands wrote the same sign for the same house.

- `measured` The template parses 68.8% of 2,162 distinct texts tightly, and roles learned at other sites raise parsing on unseen Mohenjo-daro and Harappa texts by 8 to 11 points. Class is a closed set of 26 signs tied to seal size; the name slot holds 384 different signs.
- `measured` Name-slot signs repeat inside houses (19 pairs against 7.8). `060-920` is a second kind of opener.
- `guess` Names are built from a household element plus a personal element, like family name + given name. Two openers = two kinds of owner or two institutions.

- `measured` A name is up to three signs. The first is shared by house-mates (same fish variant 15 of 23 against 36% by chance) and goes with the animal; the last goes with the class sign.
- `guess` Name = [house emblem] [self] [tag]. The marked fish signs are house or clan emblems: the fish variants are family crests.
- `measured` The jar ends the text 557 of 743 times; when a sign follows, it is the plain man sign 54 times (54 of that sign's 73 texts) or one of a few others (comb, X-with-table 679, barred rectangle 621). On round seals abroad jar and man never occur together (0 of 15); a single short stroke stands in that place, and the home house crests and name-enders are missing.
- `measured` `name + jar + man` seals are the biggest (median face 778 mm2 against 652 for `name + jar`), carry an animal 91% of the time against 78%, and run a sign longer. Spear and porter signs never follow the jar; they stand in its place.
- `measured` The ending belongs to the person: the name's last sign predicts it, the same name keeps it across seals, and house, site, period and object type do not predict it at all. The `jar man` seal's name does not match its own house's crests (4 of 20 against 3.3).
- `guess` READING: the jar is the possessive ending, `[name] jar` = "of [name]". Spear, porter and comb in the jar's place are what the person is: X the spearman, X the carrier, X the junior or craft hand. `[X] jar man` = "X's man", an agent or officer of an outside master or institution X (two earlier versions, servant of a house-mate and head of his own house, went against sizes and crests). Possessor before possessed, title after name, count before thing: a head-last language.
- `guess` Round seals abroad write a bare personal name in a foreign formula, with the single stroke doing the job of the jar. They are Indus signs fitted to another language's way of saying "X, servant of Y".

- `measured` 3 diamonds = 3 loads on 22 of 22 tablets; diamond, its second drawing and the wheel share one slot and go with nothing in the world. One cup stroke is about 9.2 litres on three measured pots; cup counts are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and never 5.
- `guess` READING: the diamond is "hand", a head counted for work. The ration tablet reads: 3 hands, 3 loads, 4 cups (about 37 litres), a gang leader's chit handed in at the depot. The seal opener `diamond + two strokes` puts the same head-count word in front of a name.

- `measured` Picture-and-slot puns work in Dravidian for 7 of 9 picture words against 0.97 by chance; 9 of 12 pairs work fully only there. Sumerian and Elamite word order does not fit the measured order.
- `guess` SOUNDS (first list, early Dravidian): jar = -kuṭi "house of"; man = āḷ "manager of a house"; fish = mīn "star"; spear = vēḷ "chief"; comb = ciṟ "junior"; load-carrier = kā "keeper"; person with staff = kō "king". `[crest] jar man` = "[crest]-kuṭi āḷ", head of the [crest] house. ## The front of a text (from `jobs/out/front.md`)
- `measured` The slot sign (002, 060, 741/742/745, 368) marks the front, whatever stands before it: on seals the name after a slot is 2.52 signs with a front word before the slot and 2.42 with another sign there; seals with no slot field have names of 3.24 signs (P 0.0003); after a slot the name starts with a crest about a third of the time against 23%. Same-frequency signs cut at instead: 1.65 signs, crest-first 11%. A front word with no slot after it behaves like no front.
- `measured` Two rigid families set by the slot: 817, 861, 824, 880 take 002; 920, 692, 550, 201 take 060; 820 takes both (chi-square 1352 against 125 dealt within site). The families differ in nothing else measured.
- `measured` On seals the front is free of the name: same name with and without a front 49 times against 49.3; one name under two front words 21 against 22.7. Front words are interchangeable on every measure (ending, count line or name, crest, impression, city, time). The slot is a marker, not a number series (after 861, 002 is 98%; 741/742/745 go with nothing).
- `measured` Tablets and seals open from separate sets (413 opens 0.13% of seal texts against 2.2% of tablet texts). Share beginning with a front: seals 28.8%, clay tags 40.1%, tablets 12.8%, pots 6.7%, copper tablets 1.1%. One front per object. With the front removed, the rest is a whole attested text elsewhere 26.3% against 17.7%.
- `guess` Seal texts come in two shapes: [field][slot][short name][ending] or [long name][ending]. The field is a heading for the capacity the seal is used in, not part of the name, not a place, not a number.
- `measured` Went against: fronted seals being bigger; one front family being the stamping family; the front replacing the crest (names behind a front start with a crest more often).
