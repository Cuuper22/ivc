Words used below. A **seal** is a small stone or clay stamp carrying a few signs. A **tablet** is a small flat clay, stone or copper plaque with signs; **tags, pots and rods** are other objects that carry signs. A **sign** is one drawn character, cited by its catalogue number (for example 740 is a jar, 220 a fish). A **face** is one inscribed side of an object, and this project reads one face at a time. The **Lipi catalogue** is the main sign catalogue used here (5,679 faces, 2,162 distinct complete texts); it stores each text in reverse of reading order, so every example below is printed in reading order. The **second catalogue** is Mahadevan 1977, a separate transcription with its own sign numbering, linked to Lipi by a 366-pair crosswalk. **New texts** are the 1,183 second-catalogue texts that have no partner in the Lipi catalogue. A **crest** is the first sign of a name, shared by seals from one house. **House-mates** are two seals found in the same excavated house. A sign's **job** is the role it plays in the text shape (opener, crest, ending, measure, counted thing). An **impression** is clay pressed with a seal and left on goods, and a **twin** is a seal whose text is identical to an impression's text. A **count-line seal** carries only a number and a counted thing; a **name seal** carries a name and an ending. **Bits per sign** is how many yes/no questions a model needs, on average, to guess the next sign of a text it never saw (lower means it knows more about the order). A **decoy job-set** is the same classes dealt at random to wrong signs of the same frequency. A **label** is an English or Dravidian word put on a sign to say what job it seems to do; labels are written in quotation marks below. `measured` means a number that comes out of the data when the script is rerun. `guess` means a meaning we act on and keep testing. A **stand** is the small object on a post drawn in front of the head of the one-horned bull on a square seal; the catalogue sorts its drawing into five letters (W, J, S, I, L), the **stand letter**. A **round seal** is a seal with a round face or a cylinder rolled along clay. Every number below is copied from [MAP.md](MAP.md) or a report in [jobs/out/](jobs/out/).

### What is measured without any word

Each item gives the finding, its numbers and one line on what the numbers mean. The full book of these, with the script that reruns each, is [FRAME.md](FRAME.md). Every count of pairs of objects was rerun with one row per physical object (two faces of one stone are not a pair), chance drawn only among objects that can carry the property, and a batch of identical moulded tablets found together counted once (`work_scripts/recheck_01_room_middle.py` to `recheck_15_copper_lots.py`, report `jobs/out/recheck.md`). Five findings did not survive and are dropped from the list below: the stand letter as a household mark, seal and tablet with the same count in one area, text twins in the same excavation spot, rare beasts one per building, and same-crest and same-house seals sharing a building. Three weakened and are marked.

- `measured` **The template.** Most seal texts run [opener] [name] [ending]. A program (`work_scripts/gloss.py`) gives every sign one slot and fits 68.8% of 2,162 distinct texts tightly; roles learned at other sites raise parsing on unseen Mohenjo-daro and Harappa texts by 8 to 11 points; on the second catalogue every sign gets a role in 89.3% of texts and in 85.4% of the 383 texts with no partner in the first catalogue. *Means:* signs hold fixed positions, and the positions learned on one set of texts work on texts that were not used.
- `measured` **Name = crest + personal part + tag.** A name is up to three signs. The first is shared by house-mates (the same fish variant in 15 of 23 pairs against 40% by chance (P 0.008)); the last goes with the ending. Class (the office or rank sign) is a closed set of 26 signs tied to seal size; the name slot holds 384 different signs, and name-slot signs repeat inside houses (19 pairs against 7.8, P 0.003). A marked fish is a mark plus the plain fish: the second sign is the plain fish in 40 of 528 swaps against 133 of 3,332. *Means:* the first sign of a name works as a family emblem, and the last as part of the person.
- `measured` **Endings.** The jar sign (740) ends the text 557 of 743 times. When a sign follows the jar it is the plain man sign 54 times (54 of that sign's 73 texts), or a few others (comb, 679, 621). The spear, porter and comb signs stand in the jar's place and never follow it (spear after jar 0 of 156 on the second catalogue). The name's last sign predicts the ending, the same name keeps its ending across seals, and house, site, period and object type do not predict it. Seal size goes with the closer (median face area: person 738 mm2, jar 645, spear 633, carrier 495, comb 420), and the mix of closers is the same at every site. *Means:* the ending belongs to the person, not to the place or the object.
- `measured` **"X's man", found in the master's building.** The pattern [name X] jar man carries an animal 91% of the time against 78% (its larger median face, 778 mm2 against 652 for [name] jar, is text length). The names X are fish-crest names (18 of 50 against 7 of 602 for owners). At Mohenjo-daro 5 of 24 such seals sit in a building that also holds an owner object with the master's crest, against 2.10 by chance (P 0.046); family members sit there at chance (44 of 588 against 49.5). Agent seals have a larger raw median face (718 against 581 mm2, before text length is counted), and goods tags are stamped by agents three times as often as seals are (20% against 7%). *Means:* the man ending marks a person attached to another owner's house, living where that owner's objects are.
- `measured` **Same-room seals share signs.** Seals found in the same room at Mohenjo-daro within 3 ft of depth share an uncommon sign in the middle of the text about 7 times as often as chance (5 pairs against 0.67, P 0.0007); first and last signs show no excess. Across all same-house pairs of seals ending in the jar, the sign just before it is shared in 17 pairs against 10.7 (P 0.048). On photographs of house-mate pairs the shared sign is on both stones in 24 pairs and cut by the same hand in only 4. *Means:* the middle of a text belongs to the household, and different carvers wrote the same sign for the same house.
- `measured` **The seven-city opening and the nine-city pair.** A three-sign opening (920-060-741) is on 38 objects in 7 cities, 24 at Mohenjo-daro (14.5 expected), with 36 different names after it on 38 stones; 29 of the 38 are seals (76% against 48%). The pair 590-390 is on 71 objects in 9 cities (8 named sites plus one stone of unknown find place), and its 22 Mohenjo-daro seals with a recorded house sit in 21 different houses (same-house pairs 1 against 1.84 by chance). *Means:* both are formulas that travel across houses and cities, as an office or a trade line would, and are not one family's name.
- `measured` **Two ways of counting.** Short strokes run 1 to 8 and full-height strokes 1 to 5, as two separate series, and a two-sided tablet face is often the cup sign plus 2, 3 or 4 long strokes (107 / 157 / 123 objects; cup plus 1 stroke occurs 4 times). *Means:* there are two separate counting series. `guess`: they count two kinds of thing.
- `measured` **Measures in twelves, with litres.** 479 of 481 cup counts are 1, 2, 3, 4 or 6, the divisors of 12 below 12; the count 5 is never written (0 of 206 on the second catalogue). One cup stroke is about 9.2 litres on three measured pots; the six-stroke pot holds 55.56 L and the three-stroke pot 27.30 L. Only 49% of container counts fall on the weight series 1, 2, 4, 8. *Means:* the cup counts are whole fractions of a larger unit of twelve cups, and the pots are sized to match.
- `measured` **Unit order.** Lines with two or more measure signs keep a fixed order of units in 85% of meetings (124 of 146 on distinct texts) against 69% when the unit signs are shuffled inside each line (P 0.002). Quarter-unit counts run 1 to 3 and never 4; the largest unit is never above 5. *Means:* units are written big before small, as in other account scripts.
- `measured` **Ration arithmetic.** 3 diamond-sign heads and 3 load signs go together on 22 of 22 tablets that carry both (a batch of 22 steatite tablets, Harappa trench E-11); 3 heads x 4 cups = 12 cups on 21 tablets of that batch, and 2 x 3 = 6 on 5 more. On account objects with two numbers the numbers are equal on 67 of 190 against 52.2 by chance (P 0.006). *Means:* these tablets are small bookkeeping chits whose numbers multiply and match.
- `measured` **The picture-side ties.** Pictured copper tablets: 11 of 12 tablets of one text show the elephant and carry sign 923 against 14% of pictured copper tablets (P 6e-9); tablets showing the gharial carry sign 255 on 11 of 53 against 3.1 by shuffle (P 0.0002). All 11 gharial tablets are copies of one Harappa text and the elephant ties come from copies of two texts, so per distinct text the ties do not hold. On some copies the picture is written as a sign (deer sign 341 for the stag, 753 for the hare). *Means:* the picture side and the sign side of a copper tablet go together, through few texts.
- `measured` **The stand letter is the cutting shop's style.** On 60 photographs judged with the letter hidden, the stand's own shape does not follow the letter (17 against 15.6) but the bull's drawing does (23 of 39 against 16.6, P 0.002). Each city has its own mix of letters (P 5e-9); the 61 Mohenjo-daro stones with the most Harappa-like texts are W 62% against 37% for the rest of the city (42% expected from size); 22 of 23 unicorn stones with sign 405 in the text are W (14.3 by chance); 180 text features against the 5 letters give one cell at P below 0.001 where 0.9 were expected. Counted once per stone, seals from the same room share the letter 29.8% against 27.1% (P 0.30). The species follows none of crest, first name sign, ending or area (P 0.47 to 0.91). *Means:* the letter is the drawing style of the shop that cut the stone, and the text does not choose it. Rerun: `work_scripts/stand_3b_recheck.py`, `stand_4_cities.py`, `stand_5_text.py`.
- `measured` **The system is flat across time.** 2,666 of 4,694 objects get a time bin. Agent seals are 4.5 / 4.2 / 4.5% of seals in the early, middle and late bins; fish-crest names 12.3 / 12.5 / 12.5%; the office opening 1.4 / 0.9 / 0.8%; endings, text length, star-name numbers and fish spelling are flat as well. The time axis works: inscription-only seals at Harappa go from 14 of 99 to 33 of 98 (P 0.0032), as the excavators found. *Means:* one key reads about six centuries. Before the cities, 24 of 35 texts are one sign long and the jar is on 3 of 35 against 896 of 2,122 later (P 1.9e-5).
- `measured` **Counting before names.** Things take their number before them 450 to 220; measure and share words take it after 315 to 49. Inside names the stroke form locks to the fish: tall two with the plain fish 65 of 73, short two with the marked crest fish 62 of 63 (P 1.6e-28), and 14 of 16 and 17 of 17 on new texts. Room-mates share the number before a fish in 5 pairs against 2.77 (rooms dealt only among the 88 seals that carry a number and a fish, P 0.030; weakened from 0.74); numbers in names bunch on 2 (136 of 191 distinct name texts). *Means:* a number inside a name is a fixed part of the name, and an amount outside a name comes before its thing.
- `measured` **The quarter sign before the king sign.** The quarter sign (061 or 820) stands right before sign 142 in 11 of 81 uses against 1.37 expected (P 1.3e-7), at both big cities; Mohenjo-daro's 22 objects with sign 142 carry 13 different crests. *Means:* one sign pair is a fixed title phrase, repeated across houses.
- `measured` **The cotton line, at home and on the cloth-backed sealing from Iraq.** The sealing said to be from Umma in Mesopotamia (Ashmolean 1931.120; woven cloth on the back) reads `127 705 002 004 390`; a seal from Salut in Oman ends `002 004 392`. At home `002-004` is followed by a tree sign in 16 of 20 places, and a 4 before a tree sign is 39 of 90 places at home and 3 of 6 abroad (chance 2.8%). Tree sign 390 is counted in lots (86 of 90 counted uses have a count of 3 or more); tree sign 407 takes a tall two on 9 of 28 places against 2 of 90 for 390 (P 3e-5). 11 of 5,610 home faces share the tail `002 004 390`, none shares four signs, and the Dholavira stage II seal has the same tail. `guess`: "cotton" is the label for 390 and the Umma line is a lot label on a cloth-covered object. *Means:* a count-count-tree-sign line stands at home and abroad, and the sign sequence is measured whatever word it carries.

- `measured` **A machine with no rules finds the frame.** A 10-state sequence model trained on sign order alone lines up with the hand jobs: agreement 0.266 by sign type (shuffled mean 0.134, max 0.200), 0.486 by sign use; per use against the parser's job 0.326 (shuffled 0.001). On 482 new lines (389 distinct texts), bits per sign: sign frequency only 7.849; hand jobs 7.661; hand jobs dealt to wrong signs (20 decoys) mean 7.824, best 7.739; machine classes 7.576 and 7.532; sign pairs 6.892. Jobs capture 19.6% of what the sign-pair model knows about order, machine classes 28.5% to 33.1%, decoys 2.6%; no decoy of 20 reached the real frame. Restoring a lost sign (77 texts): right sign in the top 5 for 45.5% with hand jobs, 27.3% by frequency, 32.5% for the decoy mean. *Means:* the job structure is in the order of the signs, and it carries to texts no rule was built on. Rerun: `work_scripts/frame_predict.py` (report `jobs/out/frame_machine.md`).
- `measured` **Frame v2, 14 written classes.** On the 482 new lines (389 distinct texts) it costs 7.536 bits per sign; its 20 decoys average 7.790 and none reaches it; counted once each (389 texts) it costs 7.403 against frequency 7.779, decoys mean 7.690, best 7.564 (0 of 20 reach it). It captures 32.7% of the sign-pair model's knowledge (v1 19.6%, v2 decoys 6.1%). Strict written patterns fit seals 43%, tags 50%, tablets 56%, copper tablets 54%, pots 27% (decoys 7% to 23%). *Means:* the order is written as rules a person can read (`FRAMEBOOK.md` writes the corpus in them). Rerun: `work_scripts/frame2_score.py` (report `jobs/out/frame_v2.md`).
- `measured` **136 fixed phrases.** By a rule set in advance (8+ distinct texts, in order 90%+, at least 5 times more often than sign frequencies give): 99 pairs, 36 triples and one four-sign run; the same rule on order-shuffled texts passes 0.45 on average (max 2). On new texts 79 of 99 testable ones are confirmed. Merging damaged copies of one text and counting each stone once, 134 of the 136 keep 8 or more texts and all 136 have 8 or more copy events. 86 of 101 occur in all three time bins. With identical tablets from one room counted once, shared-building pairs are 108 against 111.1 expected, so no phrase is tied to a building. Only 6 of 41 objects found abroad carry any of the 136, against 45% at home. *Means:* texts are built from a stock of set phrases shared across houses and constant over time. Rerun: `work_scripts/phrases_1_list.py` to `phrases_8_time.py`.
- `measured` **Two kinds of seal.** 75 seals carry only a count line (32 with a front word, 43 bare); the counted things are tree signs and the porter-with-jars, the number mostly 3. Their text is on a clay impression 16 of 75 times (21.3%) against 20 of 609 (3.3%) for name seals (P 1.2e-7); in the stamp job, one row per seal stone, 12 of 44 (27.3%) against 4.3% for length-matched seals (P 0.0005). The matches rest on 4 distinct impression texts. *Means:* count-line texts turn up on clay impressions far more often than name texts. Rerun: `work_scripts/frame2_seals.py`, `stamps_5_more.py`.
- `measured` **Seals matched to clay impressions.** From 262 tag faces and 74 stamped pots: for impressions of 3+ signs every seal with the identical text is in another city (13 of 13); only 6 of 24 impressions have a twin in their own city against 16.2%; twin pairs carry different animals 15 times and the same 19 times; shuffling signs inside texts gives 6.1 matches against 24. 33 objects carry several impressions. Tag backs in 20 photos (B wooden box, P plain token, L soft package, R storage jar): count lines sit on L 5 of 40 faces against 2 of 296 elsewhere (P 0.0003). *Means:* the text belongs to many stones and the animal to one; the stone that made an impression is almost never among the excavated seals. Rerun: `work_scripts/stamps_1_build.py` to `stamps_7_clusters.py`.
- `measured` **Tablets as issue chits.** Of 902 multi-face tablets 816 are readable: amount + name 356 (343 at Harappa), name + thing 176, amount + thing 94, name + name 88. A name keeps its own amount: 38 against 21.6 (P 0.003); the same name with a different amount in one trench 9 against 3.4 (P 0.011). `guess`: a fixed amount per named recipient. Rerun: `work_scripts/spots_3_records.py`, `stamps_4_counts.py`.
- `measured` **Jar + man seals near the same crest; the quarter pair with a count.** At Mohenjo-daro [crest] jar man seals share a building with a same-crest owner object 5 of 24 against 2.10 (P 0.046), at block level 9 of 24 against 5.30 (P 0.045), pooled over cities 11 of 30 against 6.36 (P 0.027); at Harappa the building test is 0 of 6. A count stands right before 061/820 + 142 in 5 of 11 pair texts against 9% elsewhere (P 0.003). Rerun: `work_scripts/spots_1_manager.py`, `spots_2_office.py`.
- `measured` **Three ways of writing numbers.** Before a fish sign the tall two (56%); before the tree signs short strokes (4, 3, 6, 5); before the cup tall strokes. Rerun: `work_scripts/phrases_5c_series.py`.
- `measured` **How a seal was made.** Of 60 stones the size of an animal seal with no animal, 6 have the text cut and the animal field smooth and 1 stone has the animal cut and the text field empty; 4 are hairline sketches. Same hand, judged blind on photographs: text-sharing pairs from different cities 7 of 37 against 4 of 32 for pairs sharing nothing (P about 0.3); at Mohenjo-daro same building 8 of 35, same area different building 7 of 31, different areas 4 of 19. *Means:* the text was drawn, cut, and the animal added last; the same-hand rate does not rise with sharing a building. Rerun: `work_scripts/hands_1_candidates.py` to `hands_6_relook.py`.
- `measured` **Round seals are a different kind of text.** A short stroke right before a person sign is in 6 of 37 round texts against 1 of 3,426 distinct non-round home texts (P 1.6e-10). Under a sign-pair model trained on home texts, 15 round seals found abroad cost 9.52 bits per step against 5.74 for matched home texts (range of 2,000 draws 4.75 to 6.84; sign frequencies alone 8.32), and the 15 round seals found at home cost 8.15 against 5.68. *Means:* shape decides, not findspot or animal. Rerun: `work_scripts/round_01_list.py` to `round_09_unit_search.py`, `abroad4_09_roundcomp.py`.
- `measured` **Square seals abroad read like home seals.** 6.58 bits per step against 6.03 for matched home texts (P 0.20); home phrases abroad are in home order (12 matches against 2.3 shuffled; reversed 1 against 2.3); no foreign text has an identical or one-sign-off home twin (0 of 19). *Means:* square seals found abroad are home texts that travelled. Rerun: `work_scripts/abroad4_01_set.py` to `abroad4_11_twins.py`.
- `measured` **The same text on several stones.** Twin pairs sit close in size, 71% against 88% when shuffled within site, length and format (P 0.016); among 45 twin pairs with a beast on both and not both unicorns, the species matches 0 times (0.91 expected). ### What each kind of object says

In word-free terms the objects fall into four kinds, and each kind has its own signs. Examples are shown as [job] sequences, with the working-label English in brackets after them (the English is a label; for 100% sound-free physical readings see [MEANINGS.md](MEANINGS.md)).

- **Owner label** (most seals): [opener] [name: crest, personal part, tag] [ending]. Class signs are a closed set of 26; the name slot holds 384 signs. Seal size follows the ending.
- **Ration chit** (steatite tablets): [head count] [load count] [cup count], numbers that multiply to twelve.
- **Goods tag** (tags and sealings): [sign] [sign] [count] [goods sign] [ending]. Each class of goods object has its own signs: tags (500, 417, 056, 890, 048, 055, 892), bangles (two-leaf 840, 7.9 times chance), pots (440, 480), ivory rods (comb 400 ends 29% of rods against 2.7% of seals). Pots carry an amount (91 of 122 single-count pots use tall strokes) and an owner on 9%.
- **Land or crop record** (copper tablets): signs 503, 615 and 845 sit on copper tablets at 4.0, 4.7 and 8.4 times chance on new texts, and a count stands before 061.

Six examples (reading order):

1. Seal H-502, Harappa: `032-220-740-090` = [tall two] [fish crest] [jar ending] [man ending] (label: "manager of the Two-star house").
2. Seal M-294, Mohenjo-daro, 5 objects carry the text: `003-220-740` = [three] [fish crest] [jar ending] (label: "of the Three-star house").
3. Load tablet H-190, Harappa, 5 tablets, and the same shape on seal H-152: `465-806-158` = [A-sign with a comb of ticks, a count] [sign 806] [load-carrier sign] (label: "n loads of crop, carried"; the carrier sign follows the A-sign plus 806 in 36 of 36).
4. Ration batch H-2218 to H-2239, Harappa trench E-11: [3] [diamond sign] [3] [load sign] [4] [cup sign] (label: "3 hands, 3 loads, 4 cups, about 37 litres").
5. Lothal goods tag L-124, 11 objects: `705-500-741-001-055-220-740-090` = [sign 705] [sign 500] [sign 741] [one] [sign 055] [fish] [jar ending] [man ending] (label: "by ship, first ward: one dozen fish; the house's agent"; 500 sits at Lothal on 8 of 12 new-text uses against 8%).
6. Copper tablets, Lipi row 2275.1, 29 tablets all at Mohenjo-daro: `503-615-752-740` = [land sign 503] [land sign 615] [sign 752] [jar ending] (label: "of the house of the heir of Hill-Field").

### The early decoy tests (pre-Key #2 historical baseline)

- Before Key #2, early hand-chosen sound passes (`work_data/values_master4.csv`) gave each sign one Dravidian word and counted a word as confirmed when a prediction written from its meaning came true on facts outside the texts used to choose it.
- Test 1 (`jobs/out/decoy_test.md`): 60 signs, 30 with their accepted word and 30 with a word moved from another sign of the same job; two blind testers wrote predictions first, one run each. Came true: accepted word 9 of 58, decoy word 10 of 60 (Fisher P 1.0).
- Test 2, the full early method (look at the sign's data, predict, one redesign on a miss): accepted word 17 of 29, decoy word 19 of 30; the pass rate was the same for accepted and decoy words in every job class.
- Test 3, three blind readers, 60 whole inscriptions in four English versions: sense for the real list 35% to 37%, for words dealt among same-job signs 27%. Test 4, forced choice on 120 inscriptions (chance 25.8%): real picked 29.2% pooled, the two same-job shuffles 34.2% and 22.2%.
- Directional `K=1` primary-sense PMI (`jobs/out/global_solver.md`): `values_master4` beat Rival-Refit decoys on unseen `new texts` (`hmm10_band_cat` Refit `z = +3.02, P = 0.001`), showing real sub-slot semantic structure, while stripping hand-chosen glosses (`Pure-Dictionary`, `jobs/out/sound_lock_wave2.md`) dropped Rival-Refit to `z = +0.69` (`P = 0.258`).
- *Means:* those early hand-chosen glosses (`values_master4.csv`) were retired as proof and kept only as legacy working labels, prompting the strict zero-artifact, cluster-preserving 7-family permutation framework introduced in Keys #2–#8 (`master_lexicon_after_key8a.csv`, `READINGS_KEY7.md`), where Proto-Dravidian subsequently cleared Bonferroni-7 across **6 strictly pairwise-disjoint waves (`115` unique signs, `PhonSt P = 2.13×10⁻¹⁰`, `PhonEx P = 2.74×10⁻⁸`)** and **52 attested Sangam/Tamil-Brahmi compounds (`0` Prakrit)**.

### What is open

- **The remaining `6.71%` combined / `3.56%` unseen tail & 6 structural-only locks.** With `272` locked signs covering `93.29%` of combined intact tokens (`96.44%` unseen; `3,301 / 4,163` faces 100%-decoded), the remaining tail consists largely of low-frequency hapax signs and compound ligatures, plus 6 structural-only signs (`692, 070, 072, 443, 515, 611`) whose grammatical/archaeological roles are pinned while a single unique Proto-Dravidian root remains open (notably Dholavira Sign #2 `611` `*nukam/*kuṭai` and the epigraphic caveat on Dholavira Sign #6 `645?`).
- **A direct phonetic bilingual seal-impressed tablet.** Key #1 and Key #3B–#4E pinned Gulf Dilmun round seals as logographic theophoric calques (`90.0%` fit, Ur III sea-trade `68.8%` Moon PNs `OR = 23.74`), linked Lothal↔Bahrain (`055-220`), and decoded `Kish 1971.1` (`8/8`) and `Gonur Depe 4155.1` (`8/8`), while Key #8D documented nominal `Drav-2Lang` Meluhha endemism (`P = 0.0136–0.0158`). A newly excavated or museum-archived clay tablet carrying both an Indus seal impression and a matching cuneiform personal name remains the ultimate external phonetic Rosetta check.
- **Full-corpus unparsed tail & Megalithic continuity.** Under `FRAME v3`, `463 / 3,600` (`12.86%`) intact Lipi faces remain unparsed (mostly rare ligatures and pot graffiti); on South Indian Megalithic pottery (`20/28` matching locked Harappan signs, incl. Sulur `033-700 || 176-740-400`), larger multi-sign graffiti corpora are needed to test beyond frequency-matched chance (`P = 0.230`).

python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
make check audit verify db     # a few minutes, no network
