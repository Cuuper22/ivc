# Campaign 032: `603` As X-Before-240 Bridge Candidate

Date: 2026-05-29

This note sets the research agenda for one sign. `603` is the only low-frequency sign that appears both inside a Harappa tablet pattern and at the end of Mohenjo-daro seals after `002-861`. That is either meaningful or an accident, and this note refuses to guess. Instead it writes out four rival explanations, states in advance what evidence would kill each, and ranks the source checks that would decide between them. It is a plan, not a result.

## Research Question

Does `603` behave like a meaningful sign that can move between two constructions, or is it only a copied Harappa tablet formula artifact — a shape repeated by rote in one stock phrase?

The two constructions now under comparison. "Ecology" means the study of which signs appear around a given position, and `X` marks the position that varies:

- X-before-240 ecology: `740-X-240`, `690-X-240`, and `000-X-240`
- post-closure tail ecology: `002-861-X`, especially terminal tails

## Corpus Result

Strict layer — rows with complete readings, deduplicated so one object cannot be counted twice: 4,011 rows from `metadata_filtered.csv`.

X-before-240 layer:

- 95 rows
- 28 distinct X signs
- raw bridge signs also appearing as immediate post-`002-861` tail initials: `603`, `000`

Target controls:

| sign | total occ. | X-before-240 rows | exact `740-X-240-060-692` | post-`002-861` tail initial | decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `603` | 7 | 3 | 3 | 3 | live bridge candidate |
| `636` | 15 | 15 | 2 | 0 | X-before-240 control |
| `642` | 7 | 7 | 2 | 0 | X-before-240 control |
| `000` | 810 | 2 | 0 | 1 | background/control confound |

Decision: `603` is the only low-frequency non-background member of the X-before-240 class that also appears as an immediate post-`002-861` tail initial in the strict layer. That is real decipherment-relevant structure, not a translation.

## Hypotheses

Each hypothesis below carries "kill conditions": the specific findings that would rule it out, fixed in advance so the test stays honest. A "control" is a sign or row that shares the setting but not the feature under test; a "negative control" is one that should not show the pattern.

### H1: `603` Is A Mobile Class/Role Sign

`603` may name or mark a class that can appear inside the Harappa X-before-240 tablet construction and after a closure-capable `002-861` branch.

Predictions:

- Post-`861` `603` rows should share a semantic/register pressure not shared by random post-`861` tails.
- Source-visible `603` shapes should be compatible across Harappa tablet and Mohenjo seal contexts.
- Wider X-before-240 signs should split into immobile controls (`636`, `642`) and one or more mobile class signs; `603` should stay non-background after adding source-validated data.

Kill conditions:

- Source images show Harappa `603` and post-`861` `603` are different graphic units collapsed by local numbering.
- Additional X-before-240 signs, once source-normalized, bridge to post-`861` at similar rates.
- `603` mobility disappears after copy-family or source-side normalization.

### H2: `603` Is A Harappa Formula Variant, Not A Cross-Construction Sign

The Harappa `+740-603-240-060-692+` lane may be one copied tablet formula family. If so, the post-`861` uses are separate and no bridge exists.

Predictions:

- `H-1137/H-1138/H-1846` should collapse — reduce to one piece of evidence rather than three — by source layout, shape, size, copy lineage, or transcription route.
- `603` should not have source-visible independent variation in the X-before-240 ecology beyond one repeated band.
- Controls `636/642` may show richer independent X-before-240 lives than `603`.

Kill conditions:

- `603` gains another source-visible X-before-240 witness outside the exact `740-603-240-060-692` formula.
- The visible `603` witnesses differ enough in source route and object context to reject copied-family collapse, while preserving the same sign identity.

### H3: X-Before-240 Is A Constructional Slot, And `603/636/642` Are Slot Values

The X slot may be a real tablet/seal constructional variable. `636` and `642` remain inside the construction; `603` also appears as a post-closure tail.

Predictions:

- X-before-240 signs should show internally coherent subfamilies by following material: `240-060-692`, `240-001-692`, `240-031`, etc.
- `636` and `642` should function as strong negative controls: recurrent inside X-before-240, absent after `002-861`.
- `603` should differ from them in downstream or upstream distribution, not just in site/register.

Kill conditions:

- The X slot dissolves into unrelated copied texts after source-side comparison.
- X values correlate only with artifact type/site and not with any sign-context behavior.

### H4: The Bridge Is A Cataloging/Direction Artifact

The same local numeric sign may be assigned across incompatible physical signs, or the post-`861` terminal position may be a direction/order artifact.

Predictions:

- Source-visible post-`861` rows should show ambiguous terminal sign placement or possible side splits.
- Different datasets/sign lists should disagree on `603` more than on stable controls.
- Direction reversal should erase or change the apparent `002-861-603` tail.

Kill conditions:

- Source panels preserve same-line terminal-side `603` in `M-240/M-714/M-1273`.
- The crosswalk — our table of provisional links between independent sign-numbering systems — and the source crops keep `603` stable enough against controls.

## Current Working Interpretation

The best current linguistic object is not isolated `603`. It is:

```text
X-before-240 class
  controls: 636, 642, many other X signs
  background confound: 000
  rare mobile candidate: 603

post-002-861 tail ecology
  restricted repeated tail: 533-717
  mixed bridge tail: 603
  broad/background controls: 416, 698, 000
```

This creates a real research direction:

1. Source-normalize the graphic identity of `603` across Harappa and Mohenjo contexts.
2. Treat `636` and `642` as negative controls that stay in the X-before-240 ecology.
3. Treat `000` as the background-control confound that proves raw bridge counting alone is unsafe.
4. Expand the X-before-240 class by frame and following material, then ask whether mobile X signs form a semantic/grammatical subclass.

## Source Decision Tree

1. Check `H-823 +740-636-240-060-692+`.

   This is the highest-payoff control because it is a route-only `636` row in the exact same Harappa slot frame. If it is source-confirmed, the slot becomes a stronger productive X-slot paradigm. If it fails, `636` loses one of its two exact-frame control rows and `603` keeps more weight.

2. Check `H-1845 +740-642-240-060-692+`.

   This is the paired `642` control in the exact slot. If source-confirmed, `603/636/642` become a three-value Harappa X-slot set. That demotes any claim that `740-603-240-060-692` alone is diagnostic, while strengthening the claim that `603` differs because it also appears after `002-861`.

3. Re-tokenize visible `H-1138`, `H-1846`, and `H-360` from images.

   The local numeric strings are not source-derived yet. The visible question is whether the signs occupy the same physical position after `740` and before `240` with stable identity and comparable spacing.

4. Stress-test `M-714`.

   `M-714` is the most crowded post-`861` `603` witness. If a better source image confirms same-line terminal `603`, the post-`861` side stays three-witness. If it fails, the post-`861` bridge leans mostly on `M-240` and `M-1273`.

5. Check `M-941 +000-642-240-031-002-861+`.

   This is the best negative control outside Harappa: `642` occurs in a Mohenjo row that reaches `002-861`, but `642` stays before `240` rather than becoming a post-`861` tail. If source-visible and correctly ordered, it sharpens the contrast with `603`.

6. Check one Mohenjo `636` X-before-240 row.

   This tests whether `636` is cross-register but still syntactically trapped in X-before-240, which would make it a better negative control than a Harappa-only tablet sign.

7. Only after those controls, chase `H-1137` and unknown `+000-603-091-190+`.

   `H-1137` can add or collapse a low-weight `603` echo, but it does not decide the slot by itself. The unknown scene/control row has high upside if source-found, but low route-readiness.

## Additional Controls From Class Expansion

The broader X-before-240 expansion adds controls that prevent a fake `603` story:

- `482`: eight X-before-240 rows, seven of which continue downstream to bare `002-861`, but never as post-`861` tail initial. This proves an X-before-240 sign can approach `002-861` without behaving like `603`.
- `773`: `M-706 +740-773-240-002-861-031+` has a post-`861` tail, but the tail is `031`, not `773`.
- `642`: `M-941 +000-642-240-031-002-861+` puts `642` in the same row as `002-861`, but still pre-`240`.
- `636`: strongest local negative control, with 15 X-before-240 occurrences and zero post-`861` tail-initial uses.

## Accepted And Rejected

Accepted:

- `603` survives as the only low-frequency non-background bridge candidate between X-before-240 and post-`002-861` tail-initial position in the current strict layer.
- `636` and `642` are useful controls because they recur inside X-before-240 and do not appear as post-`002-861` tail initials.
- The translation-relevant unit is now a constructional contrast, not one sign.

Rejected:

- No `603` value.
- No phonetic reading.
- No language identity.
- No translation.
- No claim that the Harappa `603` formula is source-family independent yet.
