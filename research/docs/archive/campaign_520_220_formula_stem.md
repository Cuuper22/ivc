520-220 = fixed formula stem

415     = default terminal in that slot

## Evidence That Changed The State

1. `520-220` is a real opening formula candidate.

   In the filtered corpus, `520` occurs 321 times and starts 263 rows. `220` occurs 477 times. The bigram `520-220` occurs 67 times, 59 of them at inscription start. As an opening bigram it ranks among the top recurrent openers in the corpus. This is not random adjacency.

2. `+520-220-415+` is a major exact formula.

   In exact three-sign formulas, `520-220-415` has 17 rows. In the current exact-three-sign count table it is second only to `400-740-176` with 39 rows. That makes `520-220-415` a major formulaic object in the corpus.

3. The stem can stand without the third slot.

   There are 7 `520-220` cases where `220` is followed by the end of the inscription. Four are clean exact `+520-220+` rows:

   - H-1330 `+520-220+`
   - H-329 `+520-220+`
   - M-185 `+520-220+`
   - M-978 `+520-220+`

   Three additional rows are damaged/open after `+520-220[`. This makes `520-220` itself a candidate complete stem, with `X` as an optional or environment-conditioned terminal.

4. The `X` slot is real but stratified — its contents differ by site and object type.

   Broad `^+520-220-X` rows: 52 rows, 51 objects, 16 third-slot values.

   Top third slots:

   - `415`: 28 rows, 17 exact three-sign rows
   - `240`: 5 rows
   - `002`: 3 rows
   - `016`: 3 rows
   - `003`: 2 rows
   - singleton values: `001`, `004`, `006`, `031`, `032`, `034`, `235`, `368`, `621`, `742`, `803`

   Exact `+520-220-X+` rows: 21 rows total. `415` has 17. The exact singleton terminals are `003`, `006`, `016`, and `034`.

5. The site/type split is decisive.

   Harappa:

   - 22 broad rows
   - 19 have terminal `415`
   - 16 are exact `+520-220-415+`
   - TAB:I is nearly formula-locked: 13 exact `415` rows plus H-942 `+520-220-016+`

   Mohenjo-daro:

   - 26 broad rows
   - 8 have terminal `415`
   - only 1 exact `+520-220-415+`
   - most terminal diversity lives here

   So the working model cannot treat the whole corpus as one flat distribution. Harappa looks standardized; Mohenjo-daro looks productive or less normalized.

6. The terminal slot is split between closed formula and expandable formula.

   Mechanic addendum over the 52 broad rows:

   - terminal closed: 21
   - terminal damaged/open: 1
   - medial with closed tail: 27
   - medial with damaged/open tail: 3

   Tail classes:

   - no-tail closed exact three-sign row: 21
   - no-tail damaged/open: 1
   - one-sign tail: 9
   - two-sign tail: 10
   - three-plus-sign tail: 11

   This sharpens the syntax question. `X` sometimes closes the whole formula and sometimes opens an expansion tail. That makes `X` less likely to be mere decoration and more likely to be a boundary/slot element — unless source checks collapse the singleton terminals.

7. Companion-side behavior is present but sparse.

   Companion formula classes over the 52 broad rows:

   - no companion side: 33
   - `700-034` / `034-700`: 4
   - `700-032`: 1
   - `700-033` / `033-700`: 2
   - one-sign `110`: 2
   - longer-other: 5
   - same `520-220` start on companion: 2

   This does not yet prove side grammar, but it gives the next discriminating campaign a target: terminal `415` has the richest companion branch set, while `016` and `240` touch the `700-033` branch.

8. Crosswalk pressure is useful but not controlling.

   Current shadow mappings:

   - local `520 -> P217`
   - local `220 -> P050` strongest, with `P056/P060` singletons
   - local `415 -> P092` strongest

   The local `220` middle sign remains broad fish/leaf-family compatible in source crops, but `220 = Parpola sign 60` is still not accepted.

## Ranked Live Hypotheses

### 1. Formula stem plus terminal class slot

The idea: `520-220` is a fixed stem. `X` is a terminal class/closure/status slot. `415` is the default terminal.

Why it leads:

- It explains the 67 bigram occurrences.
- It explains 59 start-position cases.
- It explains exact `+520-220+` rows.
- It explains `+520-220-415+` as a dominant closed form.
- It does not require accepting the fake `220 = sign 60` bridge.

Fast discriminator:

- Compare every `520-*` opening and every `*-220-*` frame. If `415` is only strongly attracted after `520-220`, the stem is real. If `415` follows `220` everywhere, the live unit is probably `220-415`.

### 2. Institutional or title formula

The idea: `520-220-X` may be a title, office, house, or issuing-authority formula.

Working assignment:

- `520`: frame, title prefix, or institutional classifier
- `220`: stem/head, perhaps fish/leaf-family emblemic core
- `X`: office/rank/status/branch ending

Why it is strong:

- The formula spans seals, tablets, and tags.
- Harappa exact repetitions look administrative.
- `415` behaves like an unmarked default closure.

Fast discriminator:

- Check whether longer Mohenjo-daro examples attach personal or object strings after `520-220-X`. If yes, the title/authority reading rises.

### 3. Seal-owner or identity formula

The idea: `520-220-X` may be a fixed identity formula used across owner, clan, workshop, or office seals.

Why it stays alive:

- Exact short formulas are compatible with identity labels.
- The broad fish/leaf pressure on `220` could be emblemic.
- Site variation can represent local identity formula variants.

Fast discriminator:

- Compare iconography, findspot, object family, and duplicate/copy clusters. If `X` tracks icon/workshop/family more than grammar, this rises.

### 4. Commodity or administrative label

The idea: `520-220-X` may encode a commodity, offering, batch, or authorized administrative class.

Why it is lower but live:

- Tags and tablets keep administrative labeling plausible.
- Fish-family pressure can support a commodity or offering semantic field.

Why it loses to title/formula for now:

- Square seals and formulaic Harappa repetition look broader than a simple commodity label.
- `X` does not yet behave like a measure or numeral slot.

Fast discriminator:

- Side-pairing should show numerals, measures, storage contexts, or commodity series more than personal/identity contexts.

### 5. Allograph or transcription-policy split in `X`

The idea: some `X` values may not be morphemes at all — just visual variants, source-side artifacts, or catalog policy splits.

Why it stays live:

- `034`, `003`, `006`, and `016` are exact singleton terminals.
- Prior component packets show `034/415` graphic-family pressure but not identity.
- Harappa/Mohenjo-daro and tablet/seal splits can manufacture apparent paradigms.

Fast discriminator:

- Source-grade packet over all exact `+520-220-X+` singletons plus `415` controls. If the singleton terminals collapse visually into `415` or source artifacts, the terminal-slot hypothesis narrows to `415` plus catalog noise.

## Current Decision Tree

1. Does `520-220` predict `415` more strongly than `220` alone?
   - Yes: `520-220` is a real stem.
   - No: inspect `220-415` as the real unit.

2. Does `520` behave as a broad formula opener across `520-033`, `520-220`, `520-240`, and `520-233`?
   - Yes: `520` may be a prefix/classifier selecting a head in slot 2.
   - No: `520-220` is a lexical/formulaic compound.

3. Do singleton terminals survive source-grade visual checks?
   - Yes: `X` is a productive terminal class.
   - No: `+520-220-415+` is the primary formula, and the rest are source/catalog variants.

4. Does terminal `X` predict companion-side branch?
   - Yes: `X` has function.
   - No: `X` may be internal graphic or local convention.

5. Does the Harappa/Mohenjo-daro split survive type controls?
   - Yes: regional scribal or administrative tradition is part of the grammar.
   - No: object type and publication/transcription policy explain the split.

4. Companion branch campaign

   Compare the `415` rows with `700-034`, `700-032`, and `110` companions, plus `240/016` rows with `700-033` companions. Goal: decide whether the terminal predicts reverse/short-side class.

5. Harappa versus Mohenjo-daro campaign

   Treat Harappa exact `415` repetition and Mohenjo-daro terminal diversity as competing local grammars until proven otherwise. Goal: stop averaging away the strongest signal.

## Bottom Line

The current best working statement is:

`520-220` is a recurrent formula stem. `415` is its default closing/class terminal. The stem can appear bare as `+520-220+`. Harappa strongly standardizes the `415` closure; Mohenjo-daro preserves or produces more terminal variation. The semantic field is most likely institutional/title/identity before commodity/offering — but that semantic call must be made by the next `520-*` and `*-220-*` campaigns, not by the fish-sign bridge alone.
