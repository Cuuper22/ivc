# Decoding follow-up: whole inscriptions, phonological operations, and counter fields

Research date: 2026-09-07.

**Status: executed exploratory research; no new accepted reading, pronunciation, language identification, or numerical meaning.** This report continues the integrated campaign, not its task-completion audit. It does not claim the requested major decipherment breakthrough.

## Where the integrated work leaves us

The useful surviving hypothesis is a connected spelling system: roof `65 -> 87 59`, marked roof `66 -> 87 60`, and surrounding marks `60 -> 59 211`, with corresponding marked forms on other fish bases. The observed intermediate forms and the `1380 / 2452` recombination constrain these rules together. They do not yet identify their meaning, establish synonymous readings, or assign sounds. The numerical, semantic, and linguistic models in the integrated and reconstructed completion reports do not promote a new reading.

The next investigations below therefore address pronunciation and documentary interpretation rather than rerunning the same fish-name extension search.

## 1. A new phonological interpretation of the graphical square

The square need not denote a fish word with two lexical modifiers. An alternative is that it writes two phonological features. An illustrative, unassigned pattern is:

```
plain:          Ca
roof:           Caa (a long vowel)
surrounding:    Ci
doubly marked:  Cii (a long vowel)
```

Here C is an unknown consonantal onset. These symbols illustrate a model, not recovered Indus pronunciations. Swapping which graphical feature controls vowel quality versus vowel quantity gives a rival. A second vowel-quality alternative uses a/u instead of a/i. No hypothesis of Brahmi ancestry is required or established.

This changes the research question: can a graphical operation transform a shared sound value rather than append the name of another fish, star, color, or attribute?

### Executed inference

A global phonetic key was optimized jointly across complete eligible seal inscriptions. The source dictionary is the already-held Monier-Williams XML, not a newly acquired dataset. Its extraction contains 283,809 entries and 192,482 distinct alphabetic headwords, retaining SLP1 spelling, entry IDs, printed page/column references, and source glosses.

The eligible pool is 202 distinct strict seal lines of length 2–10, covered by the forty most frequent seal signs plus the fish family. This is a restricted subset, not a claim to decode the corpus. The four phonological runs share the same 166 training types and 36 held types. Parse-equivalent strings stay together. All observations have previously been accessible; these are retrospective fit/evaluation partitions, not a blind archaeological test.

Each fish base receives a shared onset and default vowel. The two graphic operations change quality and quantity under each candidate configuration. Written 87/211 act as the corresponding operators only in the declared fish constructions; elsewhere they retain separately fitted values. Other elementary signs receive shared phonetic units. The whole key, not an independent translation per inscription, is fitted against the lexical objective.

The model nevertheless has strong assumptions: one modeled seal line equals one dictionary headword; elementary units are injective; the source language comparison is Sanskrit; and the chosen graphic/operator scope is correct. Syllable-like units are deterministic dictionary chunks split after a vowel, with a final consonant tail attached to the last chunk. These are not a complete historical Sanskrit syllabification theory. The finite heuristic search does not exhaust all keys or writing systems.

Each phonological configuration ran twelve initializations and 80,000 proposals per initialization. Selection used training score, not held matches. Scores combine dictionary-derived character phonotactics and a bonus for exact syllabified-headword membership.

| Phonological hypothesis | Training dictionary matches | Held dictionary matches |
|---|---:|---:|
| a/i distinction; roof controls vowel quantity | 62 / 166 | 3 / 36 |
| a/i distinction; roof controls vowel quality | 59 / 166 | 4 / 36 |
| a/u distinction; roof controls vowel quantity | 54 / 166 | 3 / 36 |
| a/u distinction; roof controls vowel quality | 57 / 166 | 4 / 36 |

**Dictionary matches are not translated inscriptions or correct pronunciations.** Different fitted keys produce superficially plausible words but incompatible assignments. No candidate obtained an independently grounded meaning. The result does not establish a vowel system, identify Sanskrit, or refute phonetic writing: the single-word, dictionary, unit, and scope assumptions are substantial restrictions.

### Whole-inscription alternatives without imposed vowel operations

Two other completed searches fitted global syllable-like keys, once to unexpanded signs and once to the integrated candidate expansions. Each ran sixteen initializations and 60,000 proposals per initialization. All 192,482 headwords were retained; their observed chunks supply 7,236 units.

The contribution is an executed alternative to assuming every fish-shaped sign denotes a fish species. Its phonetic assignments remain unaccepted.

## 2. A non-fish contraction candidate

A broader exact-context search, not limited to the previous hand-selected visual decompositions, finds:

```
51 130 15          / 51 130 149 342
51 130 15 176      / 51 130 149 342 176
```

Witnesses for the compact form include 2549:0 and 4649:10; expanded witnesses include 1013:0, 1619:10, and 2091:0. Compact forms with 176 occur on 4447:20, 4478:20, 4480:20, 4577:10, and 4579:10; 4666:10 carries the expanded form with 176.

Sign 15 is drawn as a carrier-like figure with a jar-shaped head; 342 is the jar-shaped sign. That makes a contraction hypothesis worth examining. It does not prove that 149 names the carrier or that the two whole expressions mean the same thing. The two matches share the same `51 130` construction and differ only by presence of 176; they are not independent lexical confirmations. No additional common complex companion face establishes equal referents. A sound equation between the bare carrier sign 12 and 149 is not supported by this result.

This remains a specific candidate for mixed full and compressed notation outside the fish family, not a decoded word or an accepted graphical decomposition. Its scholarly priority has not been established.

## 3. A source-linked counter-field alternation

Grouping objects by a retained complete, single-line companion inscription yields a useful contrast:

| Mahadevan object and face | Main face, catalogue-normalized | Other face |
|---|---|---|
| 4448:20 | 173 59 347 342 176 | 95 328 59 |
| 4452:20 | 173 59 347 342 176 | 95 59 328 |
| 5461:20 | 173 59 347 342 176 | 95 328 59 |

The first two records also share catalogue locus 41 and level -6. Sign 95 depicts four strokes, 328 is cup-shaped, and 59 fish-shaped. The count-like group stays at the same end while cup and fish exchange positions. This is not a global reversal of the three catalogue signs.

### Direct source observation

The already-held Vats plate volume was inspected at **PDF page index 199, printed plate XCVI, objects 448 and 452**. The photographs visibly corroborate the cup/fish position change relative to the stroke group. Source-linked crops and their PDF hash and coordinates are retained in the downloadable research packet. Enlargement does not recover missing detail; the low-resolution scan does not settle every fine sign variant or possible trace at the edge.

The Lipi rows independently provide these routes:

```
H-912 / H-1191, excavation reference8650c448:
  counter +220-700-004+
  main    +400-740-760-220-416+

H-1192, excavation reference8650c452:
  counter +700-220-004+
  main    +400-740-767-220-416+
```

H-912 and H-1191 share the 448 excavation reference and must not be counted as independent artifacts. This second catalogue corroborates the counter order change but **distinguishes 760 and 767 on the main faces**, where Mahadevan normalizes them together. Therefore physical identity of the main inscriptions, and especially equality of their meanings, is not proved. No new automatic crosswalk merger is made. The additional 5461 row remains a catalogue witness, not a newly established one-to-one CISI join.

A second apparent cup/qualifier reversal involving sign 28 was not added as independent word-order evidence: its recorded direction codes differ.

### What the contrast contributes

It motivates a typed-record hypothesis: a quantity-like group, a unit-like glyph, and a qualifier can have layout behavior different from an ordinary phonetic word. A candidate interpretation is `four [cup-type units] qualified by [fish-sign category]`. This is a proposed documentary analysis, **not** a translation into grain, rations, currency, fish, stars, or any particular substance.

The permutation alone does not establish number semantics, unit scale, synonymy, a silent classifier, or a language. It is useful because it gives an actual source-linked target for deciding where a decoder needs field roles instead of one uniform sign-to-sound substitution. The possibility that the distinguished main-face variant conditions the changed order must remain open.

## 4. Research direction after these results

The phonological-feature hypothesis should remain a competitor to the lexical and numerical accounts of the fish square. It avoids the unearned fish-species premise, but its present dictionary fits do not select sounds.

The highest-leverage next decoder is a **mixed, segmented interpretation of inscriptions**, with competing treatments of quantity records, descriptor fields, and potentially phonetic names or words. It should make the graphical square, candidate non-fish compression, and source-linked ordering contrast constrain the same model where their actual contexts permit. It must not assume all three are already synonymous spellings. A single shared key fitting short dictionary words is too weak an endpoint.

This is a change in the scientific question, not a claim that another generic grammar or larger model will solve it. The concrete unresolved decisions are: whether a proposed graphic operation changes sound or documentary role; whether 15 and149342 are related spellings in the51130 construction; and whether the cup/fish order is conditioned by the physical main-sign variant or reflects flexible record layout.

No new phonetic, semantic, language, numerical, or translation claim is promoted. The accumulated experiments do not meet the requested field-changing decipherment threshold.
