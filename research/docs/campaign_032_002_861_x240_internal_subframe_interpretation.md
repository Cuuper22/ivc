# Campaign 032 X-Before-240 Internal Subframe Interpretation

Date: 2026-05-29

Question: what can the compact `740-X-240-060-692` subframe mean as a linguistic object, given that `603` also appears after `002-861` while `636` and `642` do not?

This is not a sign value and not a translation. It is the next distributional object that can generate linguistic hypotheses.

## Evidence State

- Strict rows scanned: `4,011`.
- X-before-240 rows: `95` across `28` X signs.
- `240-060-692` rows: `7`.
- `240-060-692` X inventory: `603:3`, `636:2`, `642:2`.
- Prefix for all `240-060-692` rows: `740`.
- `603` total strict occurrences: `7`: three `740-603-240-060-692`, three terminal post-`002-861-603`, and one weak `+000-603-091-190+`.
- `636` total strict occurrences: `15`, all X-before-240, zero post-`861`.
- `642` total strict occurrences: `7`, all X-before-240, zero post-`861`.
- Raw X-before-240/post-`002-861` bridge signs: `603` and broad background/control `000`.

Decision so far: `240-060-692` is a compact subframe, and the `603/636/642` contrast is a count-supported slot-family object in the current strict layer.

## Working Linguistic Hypotheses

### H1. `X` Is A Slot Value Inside A Formulaic Subconstruction

Parse: `740-X-240-060-692` is a real construction, with `X` selecting a bounded class within a fixed tail `240-060-692`.

What it explains:

- Only `603/636/642` occur in this continuation.
- The broader X-before-240 class has other continuations with different X inventories.
- `603` can be special without forcing `636/642` to be meaningless.

Decisive test:

- Source-normalize `H-823` and `H-1845/H2000-4484`, then retokenize `H-1138`, `H-1846`, and `H-360` from visible source bands. If the five-sign band and X-slot survive in all three X families, the subconstruction becomes stronger.

Kill condition:

- The controls collapse: `636/642` are source misreadings, copied catalog artifacts, or not actually in the same five-sign band.

### H2. `603` Is A Mobile Classifier Or Addendum Sign

Parse: `603` is the only member of the `240-060-692` X-slot family that can also be used as terminal material after `002-861`.

What it explains:

- `603` bridges two constructions while `636/642` remain trapped in X-before-240.
- Post-`002-861-603` behaves like optional post-closure material, not ordinary continuation.

Decisive test:

- Compare source layout for `M-240`, `M-714`, and `M-1273` against source-visible Harappa `603` in `H-1138` and `H-1846`. The same graphic unit must survive across contexts, and post-`861` `603` must not be a side split, fusion, or line-order artifact.

Kill condition:

- Cross-context graphic identity fails, or the post-`861` examples are layout artifacts rather than same-line terminal tokens.

### H3. `240-060-692` Is A Semantic/Administrative Frame And `X` Marks Subtype

Parse: `060-692` is the frame-defining continuation, while `X` names a subtype or participant class. In this view, `603`, `636`, and `642` are not equivalent values; they are alternatives licensed by the same frame.

What it explains:

- `642` also appears with `240-001-692`, keeping `692` in its ecology.
- `636` appears with terminal `240` and `240-031-032-171`, suggesting it has a broader X-before-240 role.
- `603` is narrower inside X-before-240 but mobile outside it.

Decisive test:

- Profile `636`, `642`, and `603` by after-240 continuation, site, object type, material, and source family. If each X sign has a stable continuation ecology, the X-slot is grammatical rather than arbitrary.

Kill condition:

- X profiles vanish after source-family collapse or site/type/material controls.

### H4. `603` Is A Homograph Or Catalog Split Failure

Parse: post-`861` `603` and Harappa X-slot `603` are not the same sign. The bridge is created by local numeric transcription collapsing visually distinct signs.

What it explains:

- Different sites and media: Mohenjo-daro seal tails versus Harappa tablets.
- The Harappa side is exact-formula-heavy.

Decisive test:

- Blind visual packet: post-`861` `603` crops versus Harappa `603` crops, mixed with `636/642` and nearby graphic controls. The task is same-sign family classification, not value guessing.

Kill condition:

- Independent blind review groups post-`861` and Harappa `603` together while rejecting `636/642` and controls.

### H5. Harappa `240-060-692` Is A Template/Copy Family, Not A Productive Paradigm

Parse: the `603/636/642` slot is an artifact of a small Harappa tablet copying tradition. It may still be real locally, but it should not drive translation.

What it explains:

- All seven `240-060-692` rows are Harappa and all use prefix `740`.
- Source readiness is uneven: `603` has two visible objects plus one route echo; `636/642` controls still need stronger acquisition.

Decisive test:

- Collapse rows by source route, object family, archaeological context, dimensions, and plate/provenance. If the seven rows reduce to one or two copy families, the subframe is local formula evidence only.

Kill condition:

- The seven rows preserve at least three independent source/register families with the same slot structure and distinct X values.

## Hostile Demotion Rules

The bridge claim weakens if any of these explanations starts fitting better than the live hypotheses:

- Local tablet formula: `603/636/642` are only fillers inside a Harappa tablet production template, while post-`861` `603` is unrelated Mohenjo-daro terminal convention.
- Slot mismatch: Harappa `603` is medial in `740-603-240-060-692`, while post-`861` `603` is terminal after a closure edge.
- Register/material split: Harappa tablet ecology and Mohenjo seal-tail ecology explain the distribution better than sign syntax.
- Control weakness: `636/642` exact rows downgrade under source checking or are not comparable to `603`.
- Homograph/crosswalk error: Harappa `603` and post-`861` `603` split visually under blind source review.

The bridge claim dies if the evidence resolves to one Harappa tablet X-slot formula family plus three Mohenjo terminal addenda, with no shared source layout and no shared substitution ecology.
