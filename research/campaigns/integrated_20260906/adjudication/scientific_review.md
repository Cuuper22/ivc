## Required corrections identified

1. **Route A training exclusions initially retained companion faces of target objects.** The boundary evaluator removed sequences containing the held-out graphical base but did not remove other sequences on those objects. For example, holding out base 59 still admitted the marked-66 sequence `267 99 66` on object 7249; holding out base 65 admitted that object's separate bare-59 context. Base 67 similarly retained bare 59 on object 4576. The transfer evaluator retained two target-overlapping alignments for each of bases 59, 65 and 72. Whole-object and exact-expression alias exclusions are required before these are reported as held-out comparisons. Removing the identified transfer overlaps leaves 342 the leading transfer marker. Descriptive graphical alignments are unaffected.

2. **Route A's initial declared probability support was incomplete and target-derived.** Its alphabet was collected from the base/marked target records, while the all-211 model also counted training outcomes outside that alphabet, including 126. Therefore its reported distributions did not sum to one on the declared support. Route A corrected both defects using shared whole-object/alias exclusions, the fixed 1..417 plus boundary alphabet, and a common training-only backoff prior. The corrected narrow base-plus-211 model costs 135.171 bits, versus 141.524 for terminal category, 145.213 for other marked bases, 146.111 for all 211, and 323.098 for bare base. This reverses the initial all-211 winner. The 6.353-bit gain over terminal category is modest: an explicit `log2(417)` charge for choosing a marker already exceeds it. Initial boundary bit totals must not be used in the integrated decision.

3. **Route C's affine fit is an input-independent rival, and its complexity score is approximate.** The initial fitted affine model selected `a=0, b=3`: any apparent advantage is available to a smooth constant distribution and supplies no support for a count-dependent arithmetic relation. Its `NLL + 0.5 k log(n)` is an approximate BIC sensitivity, not the contract's full description length: finite arithmetic grid searches, fixed noise assumptions and categorical degrees of freedom need explicit treatment before total-cost comparisons can promote a candidate. Route C was asked to make the constant rival and scoring scope explicit. The held-out result can still state that the tested shared multiplier has not demonstrated a predictive advantage.

## Conclusions that survive

- **A:** The exact and bounded-context alignments are candidate relationships, not proven synonymy or reading equivalence. The observed coexistence of a marked fish and separate 211 contradicts an unconditional mutual-exclusion rule. It does not refute optional expansion. Missing expansions remain unknown, and no partial completion has been used as training evidence. Predicting a next-sign distribution cannot by itself identify the sign's meaning or sound.
- **B:** The complete-text/picture relations and complementary-face contrasts survive. The rejected 358 hypothesis is narrowly the universal, direct picture-category rule that demands the same category in every containing construction. It does not reject every lexical interpretation of 358, copper-specific uses, or compound-dependent meaning. Relabeling an unobserved semantic category as an unobserved production category gives the same likelihood in the specified latent models; this is an identifiability limit, not evidence that workshops caused the pattern.
- **C:** Recorded stroke groups, cross-face constraints, inconsistent equality cycles and count-changing templates survive. They preserve their explicit quantity-role and equal-value conditions. Each connected relative-unit component retains a free scale, so absolute units are not identified. The repeat/compression alignment is one observed candidate; the 24 of 120 label assignments matching it give no distinctive cardinal support. Sparse descriptor-specific fits cannot establish arithmetic when a lookup can reproduce them.
- **D:** The dictionary search generates conditional sound systems under explicitly assumed fish/211 equivalences and lexical attachments. The six defining pairs are premises, not independent validation. Candidate propagation measures application coverage, not correctness. The model-specific sound-relabeling control supports nonidentifiability for that exact objective. No sound values or language identifications are independently established.

The campaign does support the narrower witnessed structural constraints and the explicit failures or nonidentifiability of the tested models. Cross-route combination must retain each prerequisite and cannot turn A's conjectured equivalence into independent linguistic confirmation in D.
