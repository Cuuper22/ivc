## Paper 1: More Work Is Not More Knowledge

*A field report on LLM-assisted Indus-script research, May–September 2026.* 11 pages, approximately 5,000 words.

What happens when a researcher uses large language models not to answer a question with a known solution, but to sustain an open-ended decipherment project? This paper examines my Indus-script research from May through 7 September 2026. The evidence is a purposively selected record of research notes, executable experiments, source decisions, workflow controls, and recovery reports, supplemented by my account of directing the work. Models helped recover and organize a scholarly corpus, enumerate competing interpretations, connect graphical transformations, and turn attractive readings into explicit tests. They also supported a recurring failure mode: the research apparatus grew while the interpretations remained underdetermined. I distinguish productive source work from repeated verification, experiment execution from discovery, and preserved artifacts from successful handoffs. Specific episodes include a quarantined successor-thread state, reconstruction after an unsuccessful push, a misleadingly selective dictionary result, and a connected spelling candidate that failed to acquire semantic confirmation. This is not a controlled comparison of models, and the record does not support a whole-project token or cost estimate. Its contribution is an evidence-linked account of how model, harness, task design, and human steering interacted, together with a practical criterion for research progress: an action should change which substantive explanations remain possible or what observation would distinguish them.

The manuscript distinguishes observable harness affordances from model-name attribution. It does not turn an author-reported configuration or an editorial commit label into a vendor comparison. It also examines how the author's own requests for breakthroughs and exhaustive task completion contributed to the workflow's failure modes.

## Paper 2: Connected Writing Operations in the Indus Corpus

*Orthographic hypotheses, cross-face constraints, and the limits of proposed readings.* 12 pages, approximately 5,300 words.

The manuscript leads with the connected roof/surrounding-mark system, its composed application to another fish-sign family, and the conditional scope consequence of adding a literal-duplication interpretation. It then presents the non-fish `15 / 149–342` contraction candidate, the source-linked cup/fish order contrast, and the exact paired-face deterministic-lookup bound.

Observed inscription relationships, algebraic implications, proposed synonymous spellings, and actual meanings are kept separate. The connected spelling system remains a candidate; neither it nor the phonetic searches establishes a translation or sound value. Earlier same-front/different-counter observations are credited to prior scholarship. The discussion of Fuls constrains an unrestricted numerical model class, not every restricted numerical reading. Parpola's own pictorial contrasts and Mukhopadhyay's administrative interpretation are not misrepresented as discoveries or universally refuted theories.

The closing research program shifts attention toward writing decisions: overlapping spellings, physical compression, coordinated changes, copying and correction, pragmatic omission, relational language identification, and closed arithmetic relationships.

## Seven-route execution plan

`IVC_RADICAL_ROUTES_EXECUTION_PLAN.md` contains **63 ordered tasks** with shared preparation and parallel route work. It specifies scientific questions, rival mechanisms, observed inputs, and outputs usable by other routes. It includes no completion-time estimates or quantified success probabilities.

The routes are:

1. Overlapping spellings and phonetic complementation: infer shared sound fragments without assuming that every written component is pronounced again.
2. Geometry and physical compression: test whether support constraints explain fused versus expanded representations.
3. Coordinated variation: search for changes that require compensating changes elsewhere, including across faces.
4. Copying and correction: use supported transmission families to distinguish shape copying, segmentation, and possible sound relationships.
5. Pragmatics and omission: infer what information text supplies beyond images, object form, and available context.
6. Relational language identification: compare languages against a network derived without tailoring it to a preferred language.
7. Conservation and arithmetic loops: infer shared operational relationships only where the records independently support the links.

The plan integrates these routes before accepting local interpretations. It does not require a future excavation or an imagined bilingual inscription, and it does not present proposed experiments as executed results.
