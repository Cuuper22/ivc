What is the Indus script, really?

Read it at your level. The facts do not change; the depth does.

Four thousand years ago, cities along the Indus River left behind tiny stamp-seals and tablets covered in little pictures: fish, jars, people, plants, strokes. We can see the symbols perfectly. We just cannot read them. There is no ancient dictionary, no "this means that." Each inscription is shorter than a tweet, usually about five symbols. Imagine trying to learn a language from four thousand fortune-cookie slips, with no translation on the back.

The Indus (or Harappan) script is the writing system of the Bronze Age Indus Valley Civilization, used roughly 2600–1900 BCE across what is now Pakistan and northwest India. It survives mostly on seals, sealings, and small tablets. The corpus is large in count but tiny in text: around four thousand inscriptions averaging five signs, the longest only about two dozen. There is no confirmed bilingual (no Rosetta Stone), the underlying language is unknown, and even the number of distinct signs is disputed. Those four constraints are why a century of attempts has produced no consensus reading.

The Harappan sign system is attested almost entirely on stamp seals, impressions, copper tablets, and incised pottery, with strong positional regularities (terminal/initial sign biases, recurring pair and triple formulae) but extreme brevity that starves any sequence model of data. Decipherment is gated by four hard problems at once: no securely bilingual anchor; an undetermined language family (Dravidian, Indo-Aryan, language-isolate, or non-linguistic hypotheses all remain on the table); an unsettled sign inventory (Mahadevan's 417 vs. larger lists such as Wells'); and per-text lengths too short for unaided statistical identification. This project therefore targets structure first (provable positional regularities) before any sound, meaning, or language claim.

How a guess becomes a finding

Nothing is "accepted" because it looks right. A candidate must survive every gate below, in order. Most candidates die at gate one. This is the engine that keeps the ledger honest.

Gate 01

Forger & null models

Shuffle the data ten thousand ways. If a "pattern" shows up just as strongly in randomized text, it is noise. Survivors carry a measured false-positive rate (best recorded: 0.0002).

Gate 02

The skeptic pass

An adversary actively tries to demolish the survivor: remove influential rows, block copy-families, let it pick its own best alternative split. What is left has been attacked on purpose.

Gate 03

Source normalization

Stop trusting the spreadsheet. Bind every claim to the actual artifact image: token boxes, crop checks, orientation. Metadata is a lead; a photograph is evidence.

Gate 04

Blind adjudication

Independent reviewers count signs without knowing the hypothesis. Only then does it reach the ledger, with its surviving objections recorded next to it.

Eleven claims have been retracted under this process, each with a written autopsy. That is the system working, not the system failing.

Accepted · structural only

The single thing we are willing to say is true.

Think of it like grammar without meaning. We found one little phrase-ending that always behaves the same way: a specific two-sign unit only ever shows up at the end of its phrase, never in the middle. We still have no idea what it says. But we are sure about where it sits. That is one solid brick. The wall is mostly still missing.

Inside the fixed context that begins 002-861, the two-sign unit 533-717 is the only short unit (length 1 to 3, appearing at least twice) whose every occurrence in strict, source-visible rows is a terminal tail: it closes the sequence, never continues it. It is a positional regularity, not a reading.

Claim accepted_struct_002_861_533_717_restricted_tail: within the prior 002-861 branch question, 533-717 is the unique tested length-1–3 unit with support ≥ 2 whose every occurrence in strict local rows is a terminal tail after 002-861. Worst-case forger false-positive rate 0.006; strongest context shuffle 0.0002. The claim is fixed to that branch question only and asserts no token boundary, sign value, meaning, language family, anchor, or translation.

Where this sits among the other attempts.

The script is having a moment: a one-million-dollar prize, cryptographic "solutions," AI papers. This project treats all of them as comparators to be checked, not banners to wave.

Yajnadevam

Claims a full Sanskrit reading via cryptographic unicity. Not peer-reviewed; contested for an unprecedented many-signs-to-one-syllable mapping that permits overfitting.

Quarantined

Mukhopadhyay

Indus inscriptions as administrative-commercial instruments (tax tokens, licences). Published in Nature HSSC. A functional hypothesis, not a phonetic reading.

Comparator

Rao vs. Sproat

The conditional-entropy debate over whether the script is language at all. Unresolved by design: neither side claims proof without a full decipherment. This is our backbone.

Open

Nair 2026

A synthetic-baseline scorecard on the same 1,916-inscription corpus we use. Returns an intermediate result, not "language" or "not language." A null-model yardstick.

Yardstick

Tamil Nadu Prize

A one-million-dollar award (the Iravatham Mahadevan Prize, 2025) for a verified decipherment. No submission portal, no claimant accepted. The bar is real.
