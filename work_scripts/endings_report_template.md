# Endings and heads

Scripts: `work_scripts/endings_build.py` (face table), `endings_A1.py` to `endings_A6.py` (part A), `endings_B.py` (part B), `endings_report.py` (this file). Tables: `work_data/endings/`. Reading order = stored order reversed. Seals only, names from `gloss2` (`name_signs`), lost signs dropped for name tests. Ending = jar (740 anywhere, name before it), spear (520 last, no jar), porter (151/154/156 last, no jar), comb (400 last, no jar); `jar+man` = jar followed by 090. 1,100 seals with a clean ending and a name (jar 783, jar+man 50, spear 138, porter 87, comb 42).

## Verdict

1. The ending belongs to the named person, not to the object, the house, the findspot or the seal size. The name's last sign alone predicts it: 25% of the ending's uncertainty removed (shuffle P 0.003), the whole name 7.5%, the first sign 6.8%. Seal size 1.5%, object type 0.5%, animal 0.2%, material, shape, site, region, period, findspot area, house: all 0 (P above 0.29). Cross-validated logistic model, log-loss gain over always-jar: name signs 23.6%, fish-final alone 13.2%, object features -1.6%, findspot/period/site -2.7%, size 0.3%, house -0.9%.
2. So the three readings come out: free cases (a name takes any ending) is out; kinds of thing sealed (object, findspot) is out; rank by house or size is out. What is left is the fourth: the ending is the office or trade of the person the name stands for, and the name carries it. Jar = "of X": the holder, owner, householder. Spear = X the spearman (guard, watchman, soldier). Porter = X the carrier (laborer). Comb = X, a small-craft hand (comb: weaver or dresser of cloth, guess), the smallest seals.
3. A house is a mixed household: each Mohenjo-daro house holds jar, spear, porter and comb seals side by side (DKG (N) 18: jar 10, comb 3, spear 2, porter 1; DKE Trench E: jar 9, spear 3, jar+man 2, porter 2). 60 of 100 houses with 2 or more seals mix endings against 59.3 when endings are shuffled within area and 3-ft depth (P 0.65); house-mates carry the same ending 509 of 900 pairs against 513.5 shuffled (P 0.59). Two spear seals in one house: 14 against 13.0 chance; porter 6 against 5.7; comb 3 against 2.1.
4. Head of house is out as stated (part B): the head's name does not match the house crest. `jar man` is "X's man", an agent who seals for X wherever he works, on bigger seals.

## A. The endings

### A1. Do names take several endings? Yes, and less often than chance

93 names (clean, any length) sit on two or more seals; 29 of them occur with two or more endings (jar and jar+man counted as one). Shuffling the endings among all seals gives 51.6 such names (range 39 to 65). The names keep their endings more than chance. For a spear seal with an identical-name partner, the partner is also spear in 124 of 236 (52.5%) against a base rate of 13.2%; porter 46 of 196 (23.5%) against 7.7%; comb 2 of 6 against 3.7%. Pairs of seals with identical names have the same ending in 1,021 of 1,504 (67.9%) against 58.9% by chance.

### A3. What precedes each ending

The last name sign decides it.

- Jar only: 760 (51 of 53), 923 (22 of 22), 690 (21 of 21), 405 (13 of 13), 482 (9 of 9), 752 (9 of 9), 798 (30 of 32), 100 (41 of 50 plain jar, 8 jar+man), 390 (33 of 40), 176 (36 of 42).
- Spear: names ending in a fish sign. 220 plain fish 28 of 41, 233 barred fish 18 of 32, 240 whisker fish 14 of 26, 231 dotted fish 9 of 13, 235 caret fish 8 of 19: 77 of the 120 spear seals (64%), against 6% of jar seals. Also 070 (11 of 12; a vertical line with two ticks), 460 (4 of 14), 400 (4 of 15). Opening a name: 803 (12), 415 (8). Spear never follows the jar (0 of 152).
- Porter: 151 follows 097 stick-person (5 of 6), 390 tree (5), 061 (4), 416 double pitchfork (3); 156 follows 142 person-with-staff (4 of 9). Porters carry the shortest names (53% one sign).
- Comb: no sign dominates (spread over 097, 798, 390, 061, 255 one each); it marks the smallest seals and short texts (3.7 signs).

Reading, built on: a fish-name with a spear is "X, fish-spearer" or simply a house of fish-crest people who bear the spear; the fish crests (233, 235, 240, 231) lead names that take the jar as often as spear, but a name that ends in a fish takes the spear two times in three. The jar is the default: 75% of seals, and the tags that never take anything else (760, 923, 690, 405, 482, 752) are the owner-class tags.

### A5. Meanings

- jar 740: "of X" (possessive), the owner or holder: the default, any house, any size, any object.
- spear 520: "X the spear(man)", an armed office; goes with fish names.
- porter 151/154/156: "X the carrier"; the same carrier sign that closes load tags (`n loads of crop, carried`), so the seal of a porter is the person of the load tag.
- comb 400: "X, hand" of a lowly craft; the tiny seals, rods and small tokens.
- jar + man 090: "X's man".

## B. Head of house

28 `jar man` seals at Mohenjo-daro have a block-house (24 with a whole name), in 27 houses; 291 houses with seals, 1,046 seals. Chance = house labels shuffled among seals of the same area and 3-ft depth band (300 shuffles). Control = ordinary owner seals (`name jar`, 364).

| test | heads | shuffled | owners | shuffled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| seals with a house-mate | 20 | | 251 | |
| head's first name sign = first sign of a mate | 4 (20%) | 3.3, P 0.41 | 19 (8%) | 17.0, P 0.34 |
| multi-sign names only, same crest | 3 of 14 | 2.8, P 0.58 | 13 of 179 | 11.4, P 0.34 |
| whole name identical to a mate | 0 | 0.1 | 2 | 1.7 |
| any sign shared with a mate | 5 (25%) | 6.5, P 0.86 | 93 (37%) | 75.0, P 0.013 |

The head's name does not match the house crest. House-mates share signs for ordinary owners (93 against 75) and not for `jar man` seals (5 against 6.5). The first sign of a head is found on a seal of another house of the same area in 15 of 24 (owners 158 of 290): the master is not local to the room.

One head per house: 26 of the 27 houses with a `jar man` seal have exactly one, but there are only 28 such seals in 291 houses, so the rarity gives that. Bigger houses: houses with a head seal hold 9.6 seals against 3.0 for houses without, but a random seal of the same area sits in a house of 11.4 (head seals 10.5, P 0.74), and heads per 100 seals are flat across house size (2.5, 1.1, 3.0, 3.1, 2.7 for houses of 1, 2, 3-4, 5-8, 9+ seals). The `jar man` seals are not found in richer houses.

So the head-of-house reading goes. Boldest reading that fits: `[X] jar man` = "X's man", the agent or steward of X, sealing with X's name wherever the work takes him; the master's name is found as a seal-holder elsewhere (21 of 89 in `possessive_model.md`) and the seal is larger and carries an animal 90% of the time because an agent's seal has to carry the master's standing (13.5% larger at equal text length). The pattern is a master-name that lives outside the room.
